Complete Diet Guide for Budgies, Cockatiels & Parrots — Safe Foods & Feeding Ratios

July 11, 2026Dr. Pritesh Vidhate6 min read

Medically reviewed by Dr. Pritesh Vidhate, BVSc & AH, MVSc (Veterinary Epidemiology & Preventive Medicine) — 24+ years of experience in companion animal and exotic pet medicine.

70% of the illness we see in birds at Shivraj Pet Clinic & Exotic Animal Centre, Pimple Nilakh, Pune is directly linked to poor diet. Most pet owners feed their birds seeds almost exclusively — seeds are high in fat and severely deficient in vitamins A, D3, calcium, and many essential amino acids. A balanced diet is one of the most important factors in keeping pet birds healthy, active, and free of preventable disease.

Quick Facts

Species coveredBudgies, cockatiels, African Greys, macaws, lovebirds, and other companion birds
Ideal diet base60–70% pellets, 20–25% fresh vegetables, under 10% fruit and seeds combined
Common deficiencyVitamin A and calcium deficiency from seed-only diets
Emergency signsSudden weight loss, feather quality collapse, lethargy, appetite loss
PreventionPellet-based diet with fresh vegetables, minimal seeds as treats only

Why Seed-Only Diets Fail

Seeds are high in fat and severely deficient in vitamins A, D3, calcium, and many essential amino acids. A bird fed seeds exclusively will often appear healthy for years — then begin to show signs of nutritional disease: feather quality deterioration, respiratory problems, immune deficiency, and reproductive failure. By the time clinical signs appear, deficiency has often been present for months.

Ideal Diet Composition

Food GroupPercentage of Diet
Pellets / complete diet60–70%
Fresh vegetables20–25%
Fruits5–10%
Seeds & nuts (treats only)Under 5%
💡 The ideal bird diet is pellet-first, not seed-first. Seeds should be treated the same way you'd treat dessert for a child — occasional, not a staple.

Diet By Species

Budgies

  • Pellets: Harrison's Bird Foods or Zupreem — form the base of the diet
  • Vegetables: Leafy greens, carrots, capsicum
  • Fruits: Apple (no seeds), mango, pomegranate — small amounts
  • Avoid: Avocado (toxic), onion, garlic, chocolate, caffeine

Cockatiels

  • Pellet-first approach — rotate vegetables daily
  • Cooked egg — excellent occasional protein source
  • Calcium: cuttlebone always available in the cage

African Grey Parrots & Macaws

  • Cooked legumes — lentils, chickpeas (excellent protein)
  • Fresh sprouts — a nutritional powerhouse
  • Macaws: nuts in moderation — Brazil nuts, almonds

Safe Foods Across Most Species

  • Carrot, broccoli, bell pepper, sweet potato
  • Spinach (moderation), kale, coriander, and other leafy greens
  • Apple (no seeds), papaya, mango, berries, pomegranate
  • Cooked lentils and boiled egg (occasionally)

Myth vs Fact

MythFact
Seeds alone are a complete dietBirds require a balanced diet with pellets and vegetables — seeds alone cause vitamin A and calcium deficiency over time
Fruit is the healthiest food for a birdFruit should be under 10% of the diet — too much causes sugar imbalance and displaces more nutrient-dense foods
If my bird looks fine, the diet must be fineNutritional deficiency can take months to show visible signs — by then, feather and organ damage may already be underway
⚠️ Never feed birds: avocado, chocolate, onion, garlic, alcohol, caffeine, fruit seeds, or any processed human food. These can be fatal even in small quantities.

Easy Homemade Bird Power Bowl

Mix chopped vegetables, a small amount of fruit, and soaked pellets. Serve fresh daily and remove leftovers after 2–3 hours to prevent bacterial growth.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I switch my bird from seeds to pellets?
Transition gradually over 2–3 weeks, mixing increasing amounts of pellets with the seed mix. Some birds resist change — ask us for a tailored transition plan if yours refuses pellets.

Can birds eat bananas?
Yes, in small amounts as an occasional treat.

Are pellets better than seeds?
Yes — pellets are formulated to provide complete, balanced nutrition, unlike seed mixes which birds often selectively pick through, favouring the fattiest pieces.

How much should I feed my bird daily?
This varies by species and size — we provide species-specific portion guidance during consultations.

Is calcium supplementation necessary?
For most pet birds, a cuttlebone or mineral block available at all times covers calcium needs alongside a good pellet diet.

Can I feed my bird a vegetarian human diet?
Not directly — human food often lacks the specific nutrient balance birds need and may contain unsafe ingredients like onion or excess salt.

What are the first signs of nutritional deficiency?
Dull or deteriorating feather quality, reduced energy, and slow healing are often the earliest visible signs.

Do all bird species have the same dietary needs?
No — macaws, African Greys, budgies, and cockatiels each have specific requirements. Ask us for a species-specific chart at your next visit.
