How Often Should You Take Your Cat for a Health Checkup?

Cats hide illness exceptionally well, which makes regular checkups more important than owners often realize. Here's a recommended frequency by life stage.

By Dr. Pritesh Vidhate
29 August 2026 · 5 min read

In this article
  1. Young, Healthy Adult Cats
  2. Senior Cats
  3. Why Frequency Increases with Age
  4. Between Scheduled Visits
  5. Frequently Asked Questions

Medically reviewed by Dr. Pritesh Vidhate, BVSc & AH, MVSc (Veterinary Epidemiology & Preventive Medicine).

Cats are the masters of looking fine right up until they're not — which is exactly the argument for scheduled checkups rather than waiting for visible symptoms to prompt a visit.

Young, Healthy Adult Cats

An annual checkup is generally recommended for healthy adult cats — covering a physical exam, weight tracking, and a conversation about vaccination and preventive care status.

Senior Cats

Cats generally considered senior (roughly 7+ years, with "geriatric" starting later) benefit from more frequent checkups — often every six months — since several common feline conditions (kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, dental disease) become more likely with age and progress faster without monitoring.

Why Frequency Increases with Age

Many age-related conditions develop gradually and produce few early symptoms — regular bloodwork and exams catch changes before they become obvious problems, which matters more as risk increases.

Between Scheduled Visits

Any new symptom — appetite change, weight change, litter box habit change, behavior change — warrants an unscheduled visit regardless of when the last checkup was.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is an annual checkup enough for a healthy 3-year-old cat?
Generally yes, assuming no ongoing health concerns — your vet can confirm what's appropriate for your specific cat.

What age is considered senior for cats?
Roughly 7 years and older is commonly used as the starting point for increased monitoring, though this can vary.

Does checkup frequency change for cats with chronic conditions?
Yes — cats managing an ongoing condition often need more frequent monitoring than the general age-based guideline.

What does a standard checkup include?
Typically a physical exam, weight and body condition assessment, and a discussion of vaccination and preventive care status; bloodwork is often added for senior cats or as clinically indicated.
