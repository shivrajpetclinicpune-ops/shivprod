Blood in Dog Stool: Causes and When It Requires Immediate Care

Blood in your dog's stool can look alarming or barely noticeable — both matter. Learn what bright red versus dark, tarry stool means and when to see a vet.

By Dr. Pritesh Vidhate
2 August 2026 · 5 min read

In this article
  1. Bright Red Blood (Hematochezia)
  2. Dark, Tarry Stool (Melena)
  3. When It's an Emergency
  4. What Your Vet Will Check
  5. Frequently Asked Questions

Medically reviewed by Dr. Pritesh Vidhate, BVSc & AH, MVSc (Veterinary Epidemiology & Preventive Medicine).

Not all blood in stool looks the same, and the difference matters. Bright red blood and dark, tarry stool point to different problems in the digestive tract, and both deserve attention rather than a wait-and-see approach.

Bright Red Blood (Hematochezia)

This usually indicates bleeding lower in the digestive tract — the colon or rectum. Common causes include colitis, straining from constipation, parasites, or anal gland issues. It's often less immediately dangerous than dark stool, but still needs veterinary evaluation, especially if it recurs.

Dark, Tarry Stool (Melena)

This indicates digested blood, meaning the bleeding is higher up — the stomach or small intestine. Possible causes include ulcers, certain medications, ingested toxins, or more serious internal conditions. Dark tarry stool should be treated with more urgency than bright red blood.

When It's an Emergency

Large volumes of blood, blood combined with vomiting or lethargy, pale gums, or a dog that seems weak alongside the bloody stool all point toward a more serious problem — get to a vet immediately rather than monitoring at home.

What Your Vet Will Check

A physical exam, fecal testing for parasites, and bloodwork are common first steps; depending on findings, X-rays or ultrasound may follow to look for a structural cause.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a small streak of blood always a problem?
A single small streak in an otherwise well dog can be minor, but recurring streaks or any volume of blood alongside other symptoms should be checked.

Can diet cause blood in stool?
Yes — a sudden diet change or dietary indiscretion can cause colitis with visible blood, but this should still be evaluated if it doesn't resolve quickly.

Are puppies more at risk from blood in stool?
Yes — parasites and infections causing bloody stool can dehydrate and weaken puppies much faster than adult dogs.

What should I bring to the vet visit?
A fresh stool sample if possible — it speeds up parasite testing and diagnosis.

⚠️ If you notice these signs in your pet, don't wait it out at home.
