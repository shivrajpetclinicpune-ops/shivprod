Hair Fall in Dogs & Cats — When Is Shedding Normal vs a Medical Problem?

Seasonal shedding is normal — patchy bald spots or sudden hair loss are not. Learn the causes, diagnosis, and treatment for excessive hair fall in dogs and cats at Shivraj Pet Clinic & Lab, Pune.

By Dr. Pritesh Vidhate

Every dog and cat sheds — it's a normal part of the hair growth cycle. But when shedding turns into patchy bald spots, thinning fur, or hair loss accompanied by itching and irritated skin, it's usually a sign of an underlying issue. At Shivraj Pet Clinic & Lab, Wakad & Pimple Saudagar, Pune, hair fall is one of the most common reasons pet parents bring their dogs and cats in for a skin consultation.

Quick Facts

Species affectedDogs and cats
SeverityRanges from cosmetic to a sign of significant internal illness

PreventionParasite prevention, balanced diet, regular grooming, early diagnosis

Normal vs Excessive Hair Loss

Seasonal shedding — typically heavier in spring and autumn — is completely normal and usually even across the coat. What's not normal: patchy bald areas, hair loss concentrated in one spot, accompanying itching or redness, or shedding so severe you can see the skin clearly through the coat.

Common Causes

  • Allergies — food or environmental allergies causing itching and secondary hair loss from scratching
  • Fleas and ticks — parasite bites trigger intense itching, especially at the base of the tail and around the neck
  • Hormonal disorders — thyroid disease and other endocrine conditions frequently present first as coat changes
  • Nutritional deficiencies — an unbalanced diet directly affects coat quality and growth
  • Stress — cats in particular can over-groom to the point of hair loss when stressed or anxious
  • Fungal infection (ringworm) — causes circular patches of hair loss, and is transmissible to humans
🔍 Clinical insight: At Shivraj Pet Clinic & Lab, we frequently find that "hair loss" cases owners assume are skin problems are actually early signs of a hormonal or nutritional issue. A proper diagnostic work-up — not just a topical treatment — is what actually resolves these cases long-term.

Myth vs Fact

MythFact
Hair loss is always just a skin problemIt's frequently a sign of an internal issue — hormonal imbalance, allergy, or nutritional deficiency
Shaving a shedding pet reduces future sheddingShaving does not reduce the shedding cycle and can affect the coat's ability to regulate temperature and protect skin
Cats don't get allergies the way dogs doCats absolutely develop food and environmental allergies, often showing up as over-grooming and hair loss rather than obvious itching

Veterinary Diagnosis

Because so many different conditions cause hair loss, an accurate diagnosis usually requires more than a visual check:

  • Skin scraping — checks for mites and parasites under the microscope
  • Blood tests — screens for thyroid and other hormonal imbalances
  • Fungal testing — identifies ringworm and other fungal causes
  • Dietary evaluation — reviews current nutrition for gaps or trigger ingredients
⚠️ Contact a veterinarian promptly if hair loss is sudden and severe, accompanied by lethargy, appetite changes, or weight change — these can indicate a significant underlying illness, not just a skin condition.

Home Care

  • Regular grooming to distribute natural skin oils and reduce matting
  • A balanced, high-quality diet appropriate for your pet's life stage
  • Consistent parasite prevention year-round, not just during monsoon
  • Follow-up care as directed once an underlying cause is identified and treated

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it normal for cats to over-groom?
Occasional grooming is normal, but grooming to the point of visible bald patches usually signals stress, allergy, or a medical issue.

Can diet really cause hair loss?
Yes — deficiencies in essential fatty acids and protein directly affect coat quality and can cause thinning or dull, brittle fur.

How do I know if it's fleas or something else?
Fleas usually cause hair loss concentrated at the tail base, neck, and groin, often with visible flea dirt in the coat. Other causes tend to be more evenly distributed or patchy elsewhere.

Is ringworm dangerous to humans?
Yes — ringworm is zoonotic and can spread to people, especially children. Prompt treatment and hygiene precautions are important.

Will hair grow back after treatment?
In most cases, yes, once the underlying cause is treated. Hormonal cases may take several months to show full regrowth.

Can stress alone cause hair loss in cats?
Yes — psychogenic over-grooming from stress or anxiety is a recognised and treatable cause of hair loss in cats.

Should I change my pet's food if I see hair loss?
Only after veterinary guidance — an elimination diet trial should be structured properly, not guessed at home.

How long does diagnosis usually take?
Simple cases (like fleas) are diagnosed at the first visit. Hormonal or allergy causes may need blood work or a longer diet trial to confirm.
