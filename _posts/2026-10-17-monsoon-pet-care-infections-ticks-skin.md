<p>Monsoon season shifts the risk landscape for pets — more moisture means more opportunities for skin issues, parasites, and certain infections.</p>

<h2>Skin and Ear Issues</h2>
<p>Increased humidity and wet fur create favorable conditions for skin infections and ear problems — thorough drying after walks in the rain matters.</p>

<h2>Parasite Activity</h2>
<p>Ticks and fleas often remain active or even increase during monsoon, making consistent prevention especially important this season.</p>

<h2>Water-Related Infection Risk</h2>
<p>Standing water and contaminated puddles can carry infection risk — discourage drinking from puddles during walks.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Should walk routines change during monsoon?**</strong><br>Shorter, well-timed walks avoiding heavy rain and standing water are a reasonable adjustment.</p>
<p><strong>**Does wet fur really increase infection risk?**</strong><br>Yes, trapped moisture creates conditions favorable for skin and ear infections — dry thoroughly after outdoor time.</p>
<p><strong>**Are exotic pets affected by monsoon differently?**</strong><br>Yes, humidity and temperature swings can affect birds and reptiles differently — ask your vet about species-specific precautions.</p>
