<p>A rabbit's teeth never stop growing — which means dental problems are common and, left unaddressed, can affect the rabbit's ability to eat at all.</p>

<h2>Common Dental Problems</h2>
<p>Overgrown teeth, malocclusion (misaligned teeth), and abscesses are common issues, often linked to inadequate fiber intake or genetics.</p>

<h2>Warning Signs</h2>
<p>Drooling, reduced appetite, dropping food while eating, weight loss, and facial swelling can all indicate dental disease.</p>

<h2>Why Diet Matters</h2>
<p>A hay-based diet naturally wears teeth down through normal chewing — low-fiber diets are a major contributor to overgrowth.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Do all rabbits need routine dental checks?</strong><br>Yes, especially since early dental disease isn't always obvious without an exam.</p>
<p><strong>Can overgrown teeth be trimmed?</strong><br>Yes, under veterinary care — this isn't a safe home procedure.</p>
<p><strong>Is drooling always dental-related?</strong><br>It's a common cause but should be evaluated rather than assumed.</p>
