<h2>Why Ear Health Matters</h2>
<p>A dog's ear canal is L-shaped and deeper than a human's, which makes it excellent at trapping moisture, wax, and debris. Left unaddressed, infections cause real pain — dogs with ear infections often become irritable or withdrawn simply because the discomfort is constant.</p>

<h2>Signs of an Ear Infection</h2>
<ul>
<li>Head shaking or tilting</li>
<li>Scratching or pawing at the ear</li>
<li>Foul odour from the ear canal</li>
<li>Discharge — brown, yellow, or bloody</li>
<li>Redness or swelling inside the ear flap</li>
<li>Sensitivity or pain when the ear is touched</li>
<li>Rubbing the ear against furniture or the floor</li>
</ul>

<div class="post-highlight">🔍 Clinical insight: At Shivraj Pet Clinic, we see a sharp rise in ear infections every monsoon season — particularly in Labradors, Cocker Spaniels, and Beagles. Owners often mistake early head-shaking for a minor irritation and delay the visit, which allows a simple case to progress into a painful, harder-to-treat one.</div>

<h2>Causes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Bacteria and yeast</strong> — the most common causes, often triggered by excess moisture</li>
<li><strong>Ear mites</strong> — more common in puppies, highly contagious between pets</li>
<li><strong>Allergies</strong> — food or environmental allergies frequently show up first as recurring ear infections</li>
<li><strong>Excess moisture</strong> — from swimming, bathing, or humid weather</li>
<li><strong>Foreign bodies</strong> — grass seeds or debris lodged in the canal</li>
</ul>

<h2>Myth vs Fact</h2>
<table>
<tr><th>Myth</th><th>Fact</th></tr>
<tr><td class="myth-cell">A dog's ear infection will clear up on its own</td><td class="fact-cell">Most ear infections worsen without treatment and can spread deeper into the ear, risking permanent hearing damage</td></tr>
<tr><td class="myth-cell">Any home oil or remedy is safe to pour into the ear</td><td class="fact-cell">Unapproved home remedies can worsen infection or cause damage if the eardrum is compromised — always use vet-approved products</td></tr>
<tr><td class="myth-cell">Only floppy-eared dogs get ear infections</td><td class="fact-cell">All breeds can be affected; floppy ears simply trap more moisture, increasing risk</td></tr>

<h2>Treatment</h2>
<p>Treatment starts with a proper examination — we use an otoscope to check the eardrum and identify whether the cause is bacterial, yeast, mite-related, or a combination. Based on findings:</p>
<ul>
<li>Professional ear cleaning to remove debris and discharge</li>
<li>Medicated drops targeted to the specific cause (antibiotic, antifungal, or anti-parasitic)</li>
<li>Oral medication for severe or recurrent infections</li>
<li>Treatment of the underlying cause — for example, an allergy management plan if infections keep recurring</li>
</ul>

<div class="post-warning">⚠️ Contact us immediately if your dog shows a head tilt, loss of balance, circling, or sudden severe pain — these can indicate the infection has spread to the middle or inner ear, which requires urgent treatment.</div>

<h2>Prevention</h2>
<ul>
<li>Dry your dog's ears thoroughly after baths or swimming</li>
<li>Clean ears weekly with a vet-approved cleaner — never cotton buds deep in the canal</li>
<li>Check ears regularly if your dog has floppy or hairy ears</li>
<li>Address allergies early, since recurring infections are often allergy-driven</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How often should I clean my dog's ears?</strong><br>Weekly for breeds prone to infections; every 2–3 weeks for others, or as advised after an infection.</p>
<p><strong>Can I use human ear drops on my dog?</strong><br>No. Always use veterinary-formulated products — human products can be ineffective or harmful.</p>
<p><strong>Why does my dog keep getting the same infection back?</strong><br>Recurring infections are frequently a sign of an underlying allergy that hasn't been addressed. Ask us about allergy testing.</p>
<p><strong>Is ear infection contagious to other pets?</strong><br>Bacterial and yeast infections generally aren't contagious, but ear mites are highly contagious between pets.</p>
<p><strong>How long does treatment usually take?</strong><br>Mild infections often clear in 1–2 weeks with proper treatment; chronic cases can take longer and need ongoing management.</p>
<p><strong>Can I just clean the ear myself instead of visiting the vet?</strong><br>Cleaning helps with prevention, but an active infection needs a proper diagnosis — treating blind risks masking a worsening problem.</p>
<p><strong>Do certain foods cause ear infections?</strong><br>Some food allergies (commonly chicken, beef, or dairy) can trigger inflammation that leads to ear infections. See our <a href="javascript:void(0)" onclick="openPost('food-allergies-dogs')" class="internal-link">guide to food allergies in dogs</a>.</p>
<p><strong>What breeds are most at risk in Pune's climate?</strong><br>Labradors, Cocker Spaniels, Beagles, and other floppy or hairy-eared breeds see the highest rates, especially during monsoon.</p>
