<p>Cat vaccination follows a similar logic to dog vaccination — timed doses that work around fading maternal immunity — but the specific vaccines and considerations differ.</p>

<h2>Kitten Schedule</h2>
<p>The core Tricat (FVRCP) vaccine typically starts around 6-8 weeks, with a booster around 16 weeks. Rabies vaccination is generally added into the schedule as well, timed according to your vet's protocol and local regulations.</p>

<h2>Adult Booster Schedule</h2>
<p>After the kitten series, adult cats typically move to a booster schedule — often annual or multi-year depending on the specific vaccine, following your vet's recommendation based on lifestyle and risk.</p>

<h2>Lifestyle Considerations</h2>
<p>Outdoor cats, cats in multi-cat households, and cats with shelter or boarding exposure may need additional consideration in their vaccination plan compared to a strictly indoor, single-cat household — though as covered in our indoor-cats article, indoor status alone doesn't eliminate the need for core vaccines.</p>

<h2>Building an Individual Plan</h2>
<p>A rescued adult cat with unknown vaccine history and a kitten from a known breeder need different starting points. At Shivraj Pet Clinic & Lab, we tailor the schedule to your cat's actual history rather than a one-size-fits-all chart.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>What if I don't know my adopted cat's vaccine history?</strong><br>Your vet will typically treat them as unvaccinated and start an appropriate catch-up schedule.</p>
<p><strong>Is rabies vaccination necessary for indoor cats?</strong><br>It's generally recommended regardless of indoor/outdoor status, partly due to legal requirements and partly because circumstances can change unexpectedly.</p>
<p><strong>How often do adult booster shots repeat?</strong><br>This depends on the specific vaccine — some are annual, others multi-year — your vet will set the exact schedule.</p>
<p><strong>Can kittens be vaccinated too early?</strong><br>Yes — vaccinating before maternal antibodies fade can reduce vaccine effectiveness, which is why the timed schedule matters.</p>
