<p>Nutritional deficiency remains one of the most preventable, and most common, root causes of chronic illness in companion parrots — almost always tracing back to an unbalanced, seed-heavy diet.</p>

<h2>Vitamin A Deficiency</h2>
<p>Among the most common deficiencies, showing up as poor feather quality, increased infection risk, and changes around the beak and skin.</p>

<h2>Calcium Deficiency</h2>
<p>Can affect bone health and egg-laying hens in particular, often linked to diets low in calcium-rich foods.</p>

<h2>The Fix</h2>
<p>A species-appropriate, formulated diet with fresh vegetables — not seeds alone — addresses most common deficiencies over time.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Are all-seed diets really that risky?</strong><br>Yes, they're one of the most common preventable causes of chronic illness in pet parrots.</p>
<p><strong>Can supplements replace diet correction?</strong><br>Diet correction is the primary fix; supplements should be used under veterinary guidance, not as a substitute.</p>
<p><strong>How long does it take to correct a deficiency?</strong><br>This varies by severity and requires veterinary guidance for a safe transition plan.</p>
