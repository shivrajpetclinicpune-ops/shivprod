<p>Diabetes in pets is manageable with proper veterinary guidance — recognizing the symptoms early makes that management considerably easier.</p>

<h2>Common Symptoms</h2>
<p>Increased thirst and urination, increased appetite alongside weight loss, and lethargy are classic signs in both dogs and cats.</p>

<h2>Diagnosis</h2>
<p>Blood glucose testing, alongside other bloodwork and urine testing, confirms diagnosis and helps guide the management plan.</p>

<h2>Ongoing Management</h2>
<p>Treatment typically involves a combination of diet management and, often, insulin therapy, with regular monitoring to adjust the plan as needed.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Is diabetes more common in certain breeds or body types?**</strong><br>Obesity is a recognized risk factor in both dogs and cats, alongside other individual risk factors.</p>
<p><strong>**Can diabetes be reversed?**</strong><br>In some cats, diabetes can go into remission with early, appropriate management — this is less typical in dogs.</p>
<p><strong>**Does diabetes management require lifelong commitment?**</strong><br>Generally yes, ongoing monitoring and management are typically needed long-term.</p>
