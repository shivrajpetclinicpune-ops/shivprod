<p>Feline panleukopenia and "cat flu" (upper respiratory infection) can look similar at first glance in a sick cat, but they're different diseases with different severity levels — which is why an accurate diagnosis, not a guess based on symptoms alone, matters. For a full breakdown of panleukopenia specifically, see our detailed article on Feline Panleukopenia.</p>

<h2>Feline Panleukopenia — the Basics</h2>
<p>Caused by feline parvovirus, panleukopenia primarily attacks the intestinal tract and immune system, causing vomiting, diarrhea, severe lethargy, and dangerously low white blood cell counts. It is one of the more life-threatening infectious diseases in cats, especially kittens.</p>

<h2>Cat Flu — the Basics</h2>
<p>Usually caused by feline herpesvirus or calicivirus, cat flu primarily affects the upper respiratory tract — sneezing, nasal discharge, eye discharge, and sometimes mouth ulcers. It's generally less severe than panleukopenia, though it can still make cats quite unwell, especially kittens and cats with weaker immune systems.</p>

<h2>Key Differences</h2>
<p>Panleukopenia is predominantly a gastrointestinal and systemic disease; cat flu is predominantly respiratory. Panleukopenia carries a higher risk of rapid, severe deterioration, particularly in unvaccinated kittens.</p>

<h2>Why Testing Matters</h2>
<p>Because early symptoms can overlap — lethargy and reduced appetite appear in both — laboratory testing helps distinguish between them accurately rather than relying on symptom pattern alone, which changes how urgently and how the case is managed.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can a cat have both at the same time?</strong><br>It's possible for a cat to be exposed to multiple pathogens, particularly in higher-risk multi-cat environments, which is another reason testing matters.</p>
<p><strong>Does the Tricat vaccine protect against both?</strong><br>Yes — Tricat (FVRCP) covers feline panleukopenia as well as key respiratory pathogens involved in cat flu.</p>
<p><strong>Is cat flu ever dangerous?</strong><br>It can be, particularly in kittens, senior cats, or cats with weakened immune systems — it shouldn't be dismissed as automatically mild.</p>
<p><strong>Which is more contagious?</strong><br>Both are highly contagious among cats, spreading through direct contact and contaminated environments, which is why isolation of sick cats matters for either.</p>
