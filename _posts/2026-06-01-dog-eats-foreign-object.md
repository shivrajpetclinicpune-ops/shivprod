<p>Dogs, particularly young and curious ones, swallow objects that have no business being in a stomach — toys, socks, string, corn cobs, stones. What happens next depends heavily on the object and how quickly you act.</p>

<h2>Why Some Objects Are More Dangerous Than Others</h2>
<p>Smooth, small objects sometimes pass on their own. Sharp objects, string or thread (especially in cats but also dogs), and anything too large to pass through the intestines pose a much higher risk of obstruction or internal injury.</p>

<h2>Call Immediately, Don't Wait for Symptoms</h2>
<p>If you witnessed or strongly suspect ingestion, call your vet right away rather than waiting to see if symptoms develop. Depending on the object and timing, your vet may recommend inducing vomiting, monitoring, or imaging to locate it — this decision needs professional judgment, not a home guess.</p>

<h2>Warning Signs of Obstruction</h2>
<p>Repeated vomiting, refusal to eat, abdominal pain or a hunched posture, straining without producing stool, and lethargy together point toward a possible obstruction, which is a surgical emergency.</p>

<h2>Diagnosis and Treatment</h2>
<p>X-rays can identify many objects and abnormal gas patterns; some materials aren't visible on X-ray, in which case ultrasound or other diagnostics may be used. Treatment ranges from monitoring for natural passage to surgical removal, depending on the object and its location.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>My dog swallowed a small piece of a toy but seems fine — do I still need to call?</strong><br>Yes — call and describe the object and size. Some things that seem fine initially cause problems days later.</p>
<p><strong>Is string dangerous even if my dog isn't showing symptoms?</strong><br>Yes, string can cause serious internal damage in some cases, particularly if one end lodges in place. Don't wait for symptoms — call your vet.</p>
<p><strong>Can every foreign object be seen on an X-ray?</strong><br>No — some materials, including certain plastics and fabrics, aren't visible on X-ray, and ultrasound or other methods may be needed.</p>
<p><strong>How can I prevent this from happening again?</strong><br>Supervise play with small or destructible toys, keep laundry and small household objects out of reach, and choose size-appropriate, durable toys.</p>
