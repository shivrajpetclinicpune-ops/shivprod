<p>Cats are obligate carnivores with specific nutritional needs, which makes the human-food question more nuanced than for dogs.</p>

<h2>Generally Safe in Small Amounts</h2>
<p>Plain cooked chicken or fish (boneless), small amounts of plain cooked egg, and small bits of certain vegetables can be reasonable occasional additions.</p>

<h2>Genuinely Dangerous</h2>
<p>Onions, garlic, chocolate, caffeine, alcohol, grapes and raisins, and xylitol (found in some sugar-free products) are all toxic to cats.</p>

<h2>Why Moderation and Caution Matter</h2>
<p>Even safe foods shouldn't replace a nutritionally complete cat food — cats have specific dietary needs, like taurine, that general table food doesn't reliably provide.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Is tuna safe for cats regularly?**</strong><br>Occasional small amounts are generally fine, but regular tuna feeding isn't recommended due to mercury content and nutritional imbalance.</p>
<p><strong>**Can cats have dairy?**</strong><br>Many cats are lactose intolerant — dairy isn't necessary and can cause digestive upset.</p>
<p><strong>**What's the biggest risk food for cats specifically?**</strong><br>Onions and garlic are particularly toxic to cats even in small amounts.</p>
