<h2>What Shell Rot Actually Is</h2>
<p>Shell rot is a bacterial or fungal infection of the shell — it's not a single disease but a description of what happens when the shell's protective outer layer breaks down, usually because of prolonged exposure to dirty water or insufficient time basking dry. Once the surface is compromised, bacteria or fungi can take hold in the underlying tissue.</p>

<h2>Common Causes</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Poor water quality</strong> — infrequent water changes or inadequate filtration</li>
<li><strong>Insufficient basking</strong> — turtles need real dry-out time under a proper basking light to keep the shell healthy</li>
<li><strong>Shell injury</strong> — a crack, scrape, or old wound that becomes infected</li>
<li><strong>Overcrowding</strong> — multiple turtles in too small a space increases stress and bacterial load</li>
<li><strong>Poor UVB exposure</strong> — contributes to weaker shell condition overall</li>
</ul>

<h2>Warning Signs to Watch For</h2>
<ul>
<li>Soft, pitted, or flaking patches on the shell</li>
<li>Discoloration — white, yellow, red, or dark patches that weren't there before</li>
<li>A foul smell coming from the shell</li>
<li>Visible discharge or a chalky residue over affected areas</li>
<li>Reduced appetite or activity in more advanced cases</li>
</ul>

<div class="post-warning">⚠️ Don't attempt to treat visible shell rot at home with over-the-counter products before a veterinary exam. Untreated or incorrectly treated shell rot can progress to the bone underneath, which is far harder — and more expensive — to treat.</div>

<h2>What Happens at the Clinic</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Examination of the affected area</strong> — to assess how deep the infection has progressed</li>
<li><strong>Debridement</strong> — careful cleaning of affected shell tissue where needed</li>
<li><strong>Topical or systemic medication</strong> — antibacterial or antifungal treatment based on what's found</li>
<li><strong>Habitat review</strong> — water quality, filtration, and basking setup are checked, since treatment fails if the underlying cause isn't fixed</li>
<li><strong>Follow-up monitoring</strong> — shell healing is slow, so progress is tracked over several visits</li>
</ul>

<h2>Prevention</h2>
<ul>
<li>Change water regularly and use adequate filtration for your turtle's tank size</li>
<li>Provide a proper dry basking area with appropriate heat and UVB lighting</li>
<li>Avoid overcrowding — give each turtle enough space</li>
<li>Inspect the shell during regular handling so early changes are caught quickly</li>
<li>Address any shell injury promptly rather than waiting to see if it heals on its own</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is shell rot contagious to other turtles?</strong><br>The underlying bacteria or fungi can potentially spread in shared, poor-quality water, so it's sensible to review the whole tank setup, not just the affected turtle.</p>
<p><strong>Can shell rot heal on its own?</strong><br>Mild cases occasionally improve once water quality is corrected, but by the time it's visible, veterinary assessment is strongly recommended to prevent progression.</p>
<p><strong>Will the shell look completely normal again after treatment?</strong><br>Often shell texture and color improve significantly, though severe cases can leave some permanent surface changes even after the infection clears.</p>
<p><strong>How long does treatment take?</strong><br>This varies with severity — mild cases may resolve in a few weeks, while advanced cases can take months of consistent treatment and habitat correction.</p>
<p><strong>Is shell rot the same as the "floating sideways" problem I've read about?</strong><br>No — floating issues are usually related to buoyancy or respiratory problems, a separate concern from shell rot, though both often stem from husbandry issues.</p>
<p><strong>Do land tortoises get shell rot too?</strong><br>It's far less common in tortoises since they aren't in water constantly, but shell infections can still occur from injury or persistent damp housing.</p>
