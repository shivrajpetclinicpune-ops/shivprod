<p>Parrots have a fast metabolism relative to their body size, meaning appetite loss can become dangerous faster than owners expect.</p>

<h2>Why Speed Matters</h2>
<p>Birds burn energy quickly, so extended appetite loss can lead to rapid weakness — this isn't a symptom to monitor casually.</p>

<h2>Possible Causes</h2>
<p>Illness, stress, a diet change the bird doesn't recognize as food, dental or beak pain, or environmental changes can all cause appetite loss.</p>

<h2>When to See an Avian Vet</h2>
<p>Any noticeable reduction in eating over several hours to a day should prompt a call — don't wait for visible weakness.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How quickly can a parrot weaken from not eating?</strong><br>Faster than many owners expect, sometimes within a day, given their metabolism.</p>
<p><strong>Can stress alone cause appetite loss?</strong><br>Yes, but treat it with the same urgency regardless of suspected cause.</p>
<p><strong>Should I try hand-feeding at home first?</strong><br>Consult your vet first — the underlying cause needs identifying, not just symptom management.</p>
