<p>A basic pet first-aid kit won't replace veterinary care, but it can help you respond appropriately in the crucial first minutes of a minor injury or emergency.</p>

<h2>Essential Items</h2>
<p>Gauze, adhesive tape, a digital pet thermometer, blunt-tipped scissors, tweezers, saline solution, and a muzzle appropriate for your pet's size.</p>

<h2>Emergency Contact Information</h2>
<p>Your regular vet's number and a backup emergency clinic's contact information, saved somewhere easily accessible.</p>

<h2>What This Kit Is Not</h2>
<p>First aid supplies support you until you reach professional care — they're not a substitute for veterinary treatment in anything beyond truly minor situations.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Should I include any medications in the kit?</strong><br>Only medications specifically prescribed and approved by your vet for your pet — never generic human medications.</p>
<p><strong>How often should I check and restock the kit?</strong><br>Periodically, at least a couple of times a year, to replace expired items.</p>
<p><strong>Is a muzzle necessary even for a friendly pet?</strong><br>Pain and fear can cause even friendly pets to bite unexpectedly — it's a reasonable precaution to have available.</p>
