<p>70% of the illness we see in birds at <strong>Shivraj Pet Clinic &amp; Exotic Animal Centre, Wakad &amp; Pimple Saudagar, Pune</strong> is directly linked to poor diet. Most pet owners feed their birds seeds almost exclusively — seeds are high in fat and severely deficient in vitamins A, D3, calcium, and many essential amino acids. A balanced diet is one of the most important factors in keeping pet birds healthy, active, and free of preventable disease.</p>

<h2>Why Seed-Only Diets Fail</h2>
<p>Seeds are high in fat and severely deficient in vitamins A, D3, calcium, and many essential amino acids. A bird fed seeds exclusively will often appear healthy for years — then begin to show signs of nutritional disease: feather quality deterioration, respiratory problems, immune deficiency, and reproductive failure. By the time clinical signs appear, deficiency has often been present for months.</p>

<h2>Ideal Diet Composition</h2>
<table>
<tr><th>Food Group</th><th>Percentage of Diet</th></tr>
<tr><td>Pellets / complete diet</td><td>60–70%</td></tr>
<tr><td>Fresh vegetables</td><td>20–25%</td></tr>
<tr><td>Fruits</td><td>5–10%</td></tr>
<tr><td>Seeds &amp; nuts (treats only)</td><td>Under 5%</td></tr>
</table>

<div class="post-highlight">💡 The ideal bird diet is pellet-first, not seed-first. Seeds should be treated the same way you'd treat dessert for a child — occasional, not a staple.</div>

<h2>Diet By Species</h2>
<h3>Budgies</h3>
<ul>
<li><strong>Pellets:</strong> Harrison's Bird Foods or Zupreem — form the base of the diet</li>
<li><strong>Vegetables:</strong> Leafy greens, carrots, capsicum</li>
<li><strong>Fruits:</strong> Apple (no seeds), mango, pomegranate — small amounts</li>
<li><strong>Avoid:</strong> Avocado (toxic), onion, garlic, chocolate, caffeine</li>
</ul>
<h3>Cockatiels</h3>
<ul>
<li>Pellet-first approach — rotate vegetables daily</li>
<li>Cooked egg — excellent occasional protein source</li>
<li>Calcium: cuttlebone always available in the cage</li>
</ul>
<h3>African Grey Parrots &amp; Macaws</h3>
<ul>
<li>Cooked legumes — lentils, chickpeas (excellent protein)</li>
<li>Fresh sprouts — a nutritional powerhouse</li>
<li>Macaws: nuts in moderation — Brazil nuts, almonds</li>
</ul>

<h2>Safe Foods Across Most Species</h2>
<ul>
<li>Carrot, broccoli, bell pepper, sweet potato</li>
<li>Spinach (moderation), kale, coriander, and other leafy greens</li>
<li>Apple (no seeds), papaya, mango, berries, pomegranate</li>
<li>Cooked lentils and boiled egg (occasionally)</li>
</ul>

<h2>Myth vs Fact</h2>
<table>
<tr><th>Myth</th><th>Fact</th></tr>
<tr><td class="myth-cell">Seeds alone are a complete diet</td><td class="fact-cell">Birds require a balanced diet with pellets and vegetables — seeds alone cause vitamin A and calcium deficiency over time</td></tr>
<tr><td class="myth-cell">Fruit is the healthiest food for a bird</td><td class="fact-cell">Fruit should be under 10% of the diet — too much causes sugar imbalance and displaces more nutrient-dense foods</td></tr>
<tr><td class="myth-cell">If my bird looks fine, the diet must be fine</td><td class="fact-cell">Nutritional deficiency can take months to show visible signs — by then, feather and organ damage may already be underway</td></tr>
</table>

<div class="post-warning">⚠️ Never feed birds: avocado, chocolate, onion, garlic, alcohol, caffeine, fruit seeds, or any processed human food. These can be fatal even in small quantities.</div>

<h2>Easy Homemade Bird Power Bowl</h2>
<p>Mix chopped vegetables, a small amount of fruit, and soaked pellets. Serve fresh daily and remove leftovers after 2–3 hours to prevent bacterial growth.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How do I switch my bird from seeds to pellets?</strong><br>Transition gradually over 2–3 weeks, mixing increasing amounts of pellets with the seed mix. Some birds resist change — ask us for a tailored transition plan if yours refuses pellets.</p>
<p><strong>Can birds eat bananas?</strong><br>Yes, in small amounts as an occasional treat.</p>
<p><strong>Are pellets better than seeds?</strong><br>Yes — pellets are formulated to provide complete, balanced nutrition, unlike seed mixes which birds often selectively pick through, favouring the fattiest pieces.</p>
<p><strong>How much should I feed my bird daily?</strong><br>This varies by species and size — we provide species-specific portion guidance during consultations.</p>
<p><strong>Is calcium supplementation necessary?</strong><br>For most pet birds, a cuttlebone or mineral block available at all times covers calcium needs alongside a good pellet diet.</p>
<p><strong>Can I feed my bird a vegetarian human diet?</strong><br>Not directly — human food often lacks the specific nutrient balance birds need and may contain unsafe ingredients like onion or excess salt.</p>
<p><strong>What are the first signs of nutritional deficiency?</strong><br>Dull or deteriorating feather quality, reduced energy, and slow healing are often the earliest visible signs.</p>
<p><strong>Do all bird species have the same dietary needs?</strong><br>No — macaws, African Greys, budgies, and cockatiels each have specific requirements. Ask us for a species-specific chart at your next visit.</p>
