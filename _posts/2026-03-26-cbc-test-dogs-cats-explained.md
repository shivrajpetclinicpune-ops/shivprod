<p>A CBC is often the first diagnostic test run when a pet is unwell — understanding what it actually measures helps you follow your vet's reasoning.</p>

<h2>What a CBC Measures</h2>
<p>Red blood cells, white blood cells, and platelets — covering oxygen-carrying capacity, immune response, and clotting ability respectively.</p>

<h2>What Abnormal Results Can Indicate</h2>
<p>Changes can point toward anemia, infection, inflammation, or clotting problems, though results need interpretation alongside the pet's history and exam.</p>

<h2>When It's Recommended</h2>
<p>CBCs are commonly recommended for sick pets, pre-anesthetic screening, and routine senior wellness checks.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How long does a CBC take?</strong><br>In-house testing can often provide same-visit results, depending on the clinic's lab setup.</p>
<p><strong>Is a CBC painful for my pet?</strong><br>It requires a blood draw, generally well-tolerated with gentle handling.</p>
<p><strong>Does a normal CBC rule out all illness?</strong><br>No, it's one piece of the diagnostic picture, not a complete answer on its own.</p>
