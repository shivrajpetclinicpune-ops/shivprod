<p>Appetite is one of the clearest windows into your dog's health — which is exactly why a sudden change in eating or drinking deserves real attention, not just a shrug.</p>

<h2>One Skipped Meal vs. a Pattern</h2>
<p>A dog that skips one meal but remains playful, alert, and drinking normally is usually fine — stress, a minor stomach upset, or a hot day can all cause this. A dog that refuses food across multiple meals, or stops drinking water entirely, is a different situation.</p>

<div class="post-highlight">💡 Dogs can go longer without food than without water. A dog that won't drink for 24 hours is at real risk of dehydration, which compounds whatever the underlying problem is. Not drinking is generally a stronger signal to act on than not eating alone.</div>

<h2>What Might Be Behind It</h2>
<p>Causes range from mild (dental pain, a recent vaccination, travel stress) to serious (infection, organ disease, gastrointestinal obstruction). Because the range is so wide, a physical exam — and sometimes bloodwork — is often the fastest way to actually know what's happening rather than guessing.</p>

<h2>When to Bring Your Dog In</h2>
<p>More than 24 hours without eating, any period without drinking water, or reduced appetite alongside vomiting, lethargy, or weight loss all warrant a veterinary visit rather than continued home monitoring.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>My puppy isn't eating — is that more urgent than in an adult dog?</strong><br>Yes. Puppies have smaller reserves and can become weak or hypoglycemic faster than adult dogs, so the window for “wait and see” is much shorter.</p>
<p><strong>Can stress alone cause a dog to stop eating?</strong><br>Yes — travel, a new home, loud noises, or a change in routine can all temporarily suppress appetite, but this should resolve within a day or so.</p>
<p><strong>Should I try to force-feed my dog?</strong><br>No — force-feeding a dog that's nauseous or unwell can cause more harm than good. Get a veterinary opinion first.</p>
<p><strong>Does not drinking always mean dehydration is imminent?</strong><br>Not immediately, but the risk builds quickly, especially in small dogs, puppies, and hot weather — don't wait a full day to act.</p>
