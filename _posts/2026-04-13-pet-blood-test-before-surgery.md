<p>Pre-surgical blood testing isn't a formality — it's a genuine safety step that can change how anesthesia and surgery are planned.</p>

<h2>What It Screens For</h2>
<p>Organ function, red blood cell levels, and other markers that could affect anesthetic safety and recovery are assessed before proceeding.</p>

<h2>Why It Matters Even for Healthy-Looking Pets</h2>
<p>Some underlying conditions produce no visible symptoms but can affect how a pet processes anesthesia — testing catches this proactively.</p>

<h2>What Happens If Something Is Found</h2>
<p>Depending on findings, your vet may adjust the anesthetic protocol, delay the procedure, or recommend further workup before proceeding.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is this test required for every surgery?</strong><br>It's commonly recommended, especially for anesthetic procedures, though specific requirements vary by clinic and patient age.</p>
<p><strong>Does age affect whether this test is needed?</strong><br>Senior pets are generally considered higher priority for pre-surgical testing given increased risk of underlying conditions.</p>
<p><strong>Can surgery be cancelled based on results?</strong><br>Yes, if significant abnormalities are found, your vet may recommend addressing them first.</p>
