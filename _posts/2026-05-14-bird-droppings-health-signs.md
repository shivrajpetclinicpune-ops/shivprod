<p>Droppings are one of the most accessible daily health indicators for birds — learning what's normal for your specific parrot makes changes easier to catch.</p>

<h2>Normal Droppings</h2>
<p>Bird droppings typically have three components: fecal matter, white urates, and liquid urine — consistency and color can vary somewhat with diet.</p>

<h2>Concerning Changes</h2>
<p>Persistent changes in color, volume, consistency, or the presence of blood are worth noting and reporting to your vet.</p>

<h2>Why Context Matters</h2>
<p>Some changes are temporarily diet-related; persistent changes not explained by food are the ones that need evaluation.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can food color affect dropping color?</strong><br>Yes, temporarily — this isn't necessarily concerning on its own.</p>
<p><strong>Should I photograph unusual droppings?</strong><br>Yes, this can help your vet assess changes over time or during a call.</p>
<p><strong>Is any blood in droppings normal?</strong><br>No, this should always be evaluated.</p>
