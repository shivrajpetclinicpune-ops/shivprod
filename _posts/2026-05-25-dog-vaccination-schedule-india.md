<p>Vaccination confusion is one of the most common reasons puppies end up under-protected — not because owners don't care, but because schedules get missed, delayed, or misunderstood. Here's a straightforward, age-by-age breakdown of what a dog vaccination schedule in India typically looks like, and why timing matters as much as the vaccine itself.</p>

<h2>Why Timing Matters</h2>
<p>Puppies are born with some temporary immunity from their mother, which fades gradually over the first weeks of life. Vaccinating too early means the maternal antibodies can interfere with the vaccine; vaccinating too late leaves a gap where the puppy has no protection at all. This is why a structured, multi-dose schedule — not a single shot — is standard.</p>

<h2>The Puppy Schedule</h2>
<p>Generally, the first core vaccine (often called Puppy DP, covering Distemper and Parvovirus) is given around 6-8 weeks, with follow-up doses every 2-4 weeks until the puppy reaches roughly 16 weeks. The core combination (commonly DHPPi/DHLPPi) continues through this window, alongside the rabies vaccine, usually starting around 14-16 weeks.</p>

<h2>Adult Boosters</h2>
<p>Once the puppy series is complete, adult dogs move to a booster schedule — commonly annual for the core combination and leptospirosis, with rabies boosters following your local regulations (often every 1-3 years). Lifestyle vaccines like Bordetella (kennel cough) are added if your dog socializes frequently at parks, boarding, or grooming facilities.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>My puppy missed a scheduled dose — do we start over?</strong><br>Not usually — your vet can typically resume from where you left off, though the exact catch-up plan depends on how much time has passed.</p>
<p><strong>Is the rabies vaccine legally required in India?</strong><br>Rabies vaccination is strongly recommended and often required under local regulations; ask your vet about current requirements in your area.</p>
<p><strong>Does an indoor dog need the full schedule?</strong><br>Yes. Several core diseases spread through contaminated environments and objects, not just direct contact with other dogs, so indoor dogs still need protection.</p>
<p><strong>When should adult boosters start?</strong><br>Typically one year after the puppy series completes, then on an annual or multi-year cycle depending on the specific vaccine.</p>
