Blood in a cat's urine usually comes from inflammation of the bladder, a group of problems vets call **FLUTD** (feline lower urinary tract disease). Most cases are painful but not immediately life-threatening. One situation is different: a cat, almost always a **male**, whose urethra is blocked and who cannot pass urine at all.

## First, rule out a blockage

<div class="post-warning"><strong>Emergency signs, especially in male cats:</strong> repeated trips to the litter box with only drops or no urine, crying while straining, licking the penis, hiding, vomiting, or a cat that becomes quiet and stops eating after straining. A blocked cat's potassium rises and can stop the heart, usually within 24–72 hours. Come in immediately during clinic hours. After hours or on a Wednesday, go to a 24-hour veterinary hospital. Don't wait until morning.</div>

Owners often mistake straining to urinate for **constipation**. If you can't be certain urine is coming out, treat it as a blockage.

Check the litter: are there normal-sized clumps? A male cat producing only small spots, or wet patches with blood, needs to be seen the same day.

## Common causes of blood in the urine

**Idiopathic cystitis (FIC):** the most common cause in young and middle-aged cats. There's no infection. It is bladder inflammation closely linked to **stress**: a new pet, moving house, visitors, Diwali crackers, construction noise, a dirty or shared litter box, or a change in routine. It tends to come back in episodes.

**Urinary crystals and stones:** struvite and calcium oxalate. Crystals can form a sandy "plug" that blocks the narrow male urethra. Diet and low water intake play a big role.

**Bacterial infection:** less common in young cats than people assume, but more common in older cats, especially those with kidney disease or diabetes.

**Less common causes:** bladder tumours in older cats, trauma (a fall from a balcony), and clotting problems from rat poison.

## What we do at the clinic

- **Gentle abdominal palpation.** A blocked bladder is large, hard and painful.
- **Urinalysis** in our in-house lab, to look for blood, crystals, bacteria and urine concentration.
- **Blood tests (biochemistry and electrolytes)** in about 15–20 minutes if the cat is blocked or unwell, to check kidney values and the dangerous potassium level.
- **Digital X-ray** to look for stones in the bladder or urethra.

**If the cat is blocked**, we stabilise it first (fluids, pain relief, correcting potassium), then relieve the blockage under sedation or anaesthesia with a urinary catheter. Most cats need hospital care with the catheter in place for a period afterwards. Cats that block repeatedly despite diet change may need a surgery called perineal urethrostomy.

**If not blocked**, treatment is pain relief, anti-inflammatories, more water and stress reduction. Antibiotics are used only when infection is confirmed.

## Preventing repeat episodes

1. **More water.** Wet food is the biggest single change. Add water to wet food, use a water fountain, and keep several bowls away from the food.
2. **Prescription urinary diet** when crystals or stones were found. It only works if it is the *only* food. No mixing with other foods.
3. **Litter boxes:** one per cat plus one, scooped twice daily, in quiet places. Many indoor flat cats hold urine because the box is dirty or in a noisy spot.
4. **Lower stress:** hiding places, high shelves, play sessions, and a calm, predictable routine. Plan ahead for Diwali.
5. **Weight control.** Overweight, inactive indoor males are at the highest risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do female cats get blocked?**\
Very rarely, because their urethra is wider. Females still get cystitis and need treatment for pain and bloody urine.

**Will my cat need a lifelong special diet?**\
If stones or recurring crystals were found, usually yes, or at least long-term. For stress-related cystitis, more water and less stress may be enough.

**Can I give my cat cranberry or a painkiller from home?**\
No. Human painkillers, especially paracetamol, are deadly to cats, and cranberry doesn't treat feline cystitis.
