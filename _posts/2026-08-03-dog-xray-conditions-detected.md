<p>X-rays remain one of the most commonly used and versatile diagnostic tools for dogs, useful across a wide range of conditions.</p>

<h2>Skeletal Conditions</h2>
<p>Fractures, joint disease, and arthritis are commonly evaluated using X-rays.</p>

<h2>Chest and Abdominal Findings</h2>
<p>Heart size, lung abnormalities, certain foreign objects, and some urinary stones can be identified on X-ray.</p>

<h2>Limitations</h2>
<p>Not every condition or object is visible on X-ray — soft tissue detail is often better assessed with ultrasound, as covered in our imaging comparison article.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is sedation always needed for a dog X-ray?</strong><br>Not always — this depends on the pet's temperament and the positioning required.</p>
<p><strong>Can X-rays detect all swallowed objects?</strong><br>No, some materials aren't visible on X-ray — further diagnostics may be needed.</p>
<p><strong>How safe is X-ray radiation for dogs?</strong><br>Modern veterinary X-ray equipment uses controlled, low radiation levels considered safe when used appropriately.</p>
