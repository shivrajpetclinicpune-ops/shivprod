<p>The first 30 days set the tone for your pet's long-term health and behavior — a structured approach helps you cover what matters without feeling overwhelmed.</p>

<h2>Week 1: Settling In and First Vet Visit</h2>
<p>Schedule an initial veterinary visit promptly to establish health baseline, vaccination status, and any immediate concerns.</p>

<h2>Weeks 2-3: Building Routine</h2>
<p>Establish consistent feeding, exercise, and sleep routines — pets generally settle in faster with predictable structure.</p>

<h2>Week 4: Preventive Care Follow-Through</h2>
<p>Ensure vaccination and deworming schedules are on track, and address any behavior or health questions that have emerged during settling-in.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Is the first vet visit urgent even if the pet seems healthy?**</strong><br>Yes, establishing a baseline and vaccination status early is important regardless of apparent health.</p>
<p><strong>**Should I introduce a new pet to other household pets gradually?**</strong><br>Yes, gradual, supervised introductions generally work better than immediate full contact.</p>
<p><strong>**What if I'm unsure about my new pet's medical history?**</strong><br>Share what you know with your vet, who can build an appropriate plan around any gaps.</p>
