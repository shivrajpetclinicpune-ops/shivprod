When a dog is unwell, the physical examination tells us a lot, but it can't show low platelets, failing kidneys or a dangerous electrolyte imbalance. Blood tests fill that gap. The aim is not to run everything. It is to run the tests whose answers will change what we do **today**.

At Shivraj Pet Clinic & Lab, CBC and biochemistry are processed in our in-house lab, with results in about **15–20 minutes**.

## The core panel

**Complete blood count (CBC)**
- **Platelets:** low platelets are the most common lab clue to **tick fever** (*Ehrlichia*, *Anaplasma*) in Pune and PCMC dogs. We check this in almost every dog with a fever.
- **Red cells:** anaemia from *Babesia*, hookworms, bleeding, or chronic disease.
- **White cells:** very high counts can mean a serious bacterial infection, such as **pyometra** (infected uterus) in an unspayed female. Very low counts in a vomiting puppy suggest **parvovirus**.

**Biochemistry**
- **Kidney values:** dehydration, kidney infection or failure, and leptospirosis after the monsoon.
- **Liver values:** hepatitis, toxins, tick fever, or medicine side effects.
- **Glucose:** diabetes (high) or sepsis and toy-breed puppy hypoglycaemia (low).
- **Proteins:** low albumin with diarrhoea or kidney disease; high globulin in chronic infections.
- **Electrolytes:** corrected with the right fluids in vomiting and diarrhoea.

## What we add for specific problems

| Symptom | Likely additions |
|---|---|
| Fever, dullness, low appetite | **Blood smear** for *Babesia* and *Ehrlichia*; rapid tick-fever antibody kit if needed |
| Vomiting or bloody diarrhoea in a puppy | Parvovirus rapid test, faecal exam |
| Drinking and urinating much more | Urinalysis with urine concentration; cortisol tests (sent out) if Cushing's is suspected |
| Jaundice (yellow gums or eyes) | Bilirubin, liver panel, blood smear, leptospirosis testing |
| Bleeding, bruises, nosebleed | Platelets, smear; clotting assessment if rat poison is possible |
| Unspayed female, off food, drinking more, weeks after heat | CBC + **ultrasound or X-ray** for pyometra |
| Weakness after the monsoon, red eyes, jaundice | Leptospirosis tests, kidney and liver values |

## Why same-visit results matter

Imagine a 4-year-old Labrador from Wakad with fever and no appetite. Without testing, the usual home "fever injection" may bring the temperature down for a day. With a CBC in the clinic, we may see a platelet count far below normal. That changes everything: the dog starts the right antibiotic today, avoids medicines that worsen bleeding, and the owner knows exactly what warning signs to watch for.

## Before you come in

- For routine checks, fast your dog for about 8–12 hours (water is fine). For a dog that is already sick and not eating, come in as you are.
- Bring a list of all medicines given in the last two weeks, including home remedies and anything from a pharmacy.
- Bring previous reports. A trend is often more informative than a single value.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do you need to repeat blood tests during treatment?**\
Often, yes. In tick fever, a repeat CBC shows whether platelets are recovering. In kidney problems, repeat kidney values guide how long fluids continue.

**My dog is scared of needles. Is blood collection painful?**\
It's a quick prick, similar to a vaccination. We use gentle restraint and treats, and most dogs barely notice.
