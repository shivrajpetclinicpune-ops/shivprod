<p>“My cat never goes outside” is the single most common reason owners skip or delay the Tricat vaccine — and it's also the reasoning that leaves cats exposed to diseases that don't require outdoor contact to spread.</p>

<h2>What Tricat Protects Against</h2>
<p>The Tricat vaccine (also called FVRCP) protects against three significant feline diseases: feline viral rhinotracheitis, calicivirus, and feline panleukopenia. Panleukopenia in particular is a severe, often life-threatening disease that we cover in more depth elsewhere on this site.</p>

<h2>Why Indoor Cats Still Need It</h2>
<p>Viruses don't need your cat to go outside to reach them. They can travel in on shoes, clothing, a visitor's hands, a newly adopted animal, or a contaminated carrier. Circumstances also change — an indoor cat can escape, need boarding, or require hospitalization, all of which introduce exposure risk.</p>

<h2>Kitten Schedule</h2>
<p>Kittens typically receive their first Tricat dose around 6-8 weeks, with a booster around 16 weeks, timed to work around fading maternal antibodies — similar logic to puppy vaccination timing. Adult cats generally move to a booster schedule after the kitten series.</p>

<div class="post-highlight">💡 Households with more than one cat, or cats that visit boarding, grooming, or shelter environments, face higher exposure risk. A fully vaccinated cat in a multi-cat home also protects the household as a whole, since disease spreads faster where animals live close together.</div>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can a vaccinated cat still get panleukopenia?</strong><br>Vaccination greatly reduces risk but no vaccine offers 100% protection — proper vaccination remains the best defense available.</p>
<p><strong>Does my cat need Tricat if she's never left the house?</strong><br>Yes — the virus can be carried in on shoes, clothing, or visitors, and circumstances like boarding or escape can change exposure risk at any time.</p>
<p><strong>How often does the booster need to be repeated?</strong><br>Typically annually or on a multi-year cycle depending on the specific vaccine protocol your vet recommends.</p>
<p><strong>Is Tricat the same as FVRCP?</strong><br>Yes — Tricat is the commonly used name in India for the FVRCP combination vaccine.</p>
