<p>In an emergency, what you tell your vet in the first minute can shape how quickly appropriate treatment begins.</p>

<h2>Core Information</h2>
<p>Your pet's weight, what symptoms you're seeing, when they started, and any known exposure to toxins, injury, or trauma.</p>

<h2>If Poisoning Is Suspected</h2>
<p>Bring packaging or identify the substance and estimated amount if at all possible — this significantly speeds up appropriate treatment.</p>

<h2>Recent Changes</h2>
<p>Mention any recent medication changes, new foods, or environmental changes that might be relevant.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Should I call ahead if possible?</strong><br>Yes, this lets the clinic prepare for your pet's specific situation before you arrive.</p>
<p><strong>What if I don't know exactly what happened?</strong><br>Share whatever you do know — partial information is still valuable, and your vet will guide further questions.</p>
<p><strong>Is it helpful to bring photos of symptoms?</strong><br>Yes, especially for things like skin changes or vomit/stool appearance that may change by the time you arrive.</p>
