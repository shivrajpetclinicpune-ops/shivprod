<p>Birds' unique respiratory anatomy — lungs plus air sacs — means respiratory disease affects them differently, and often more seriously, than mammals.</p>

<h2>Why Birds Are Vulnerable</h2>
<p>The efficient but delicate air-sac system means infections or irritants can spread and impact breathing capacity more significantly than in mammals.</p>

<h2>Common Causes</h2>
<p>Bacterial and fungal infections, nutritional deficiency (particularly Vitamin A), and environmental irritants like smoke or fumes are common contributors.</p>

<h2>Recognizing It Early</h2>
<p>Tail bobbing, open-mouth breathing, and voice changes are early signs worth acting on immediately, not monitoring.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Are certain environments riskier for bird respiratory health?</strong><br>Yes, smoke, aerosols, and certain cookware fumes are known risks.</p>
<p><strong>Can diet affect respiratory disease risk?</strong><br>Yes, Vitamin A deficiency specifically is linked to increased respiratory vulnerability.</p>
<p><strong>Is respiratory disease in birds always an emergency?</strong><br>Any visible respiratory distress should be treated as urgent given how quickly birds can decline.</p>
