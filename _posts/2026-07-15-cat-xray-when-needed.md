<p>Cats can need X-rays for many of the same reasons as dogs, though certain conditions — like urinary blockages — are especially relevant to this species.</p>

<h2>Common Reasons</h2>
<p>Trauma, suspected fractures, breathing difficulty, urinary stones, and unexplained illness are common reasons X-rays are recommended for cats.</p>

<h2>Urinary-Specific Value</h2>
<p>Given how common urinary issues are in cats, X-rays can play a role in identifying stones contributing to blockage risk.</p>

<h2>What to Expect</h2>
<p>Cats are generally positioned carefully and calmly for imaging; sedation may be used if needed for comfort and image quality.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Does my cat need sedation for an X-ray?</strong><br>Not always, but it may be recommended depending on your cat's temperament and the imaging needed.</p>
<p><strong>Can X-rays detect all bladder stones?</strong><br>Some stone types are more visible than others — ultrasound may be used alongside for a fuller picture.</p>
<p><strong>Is imaging safe for pregnant cats?</strong><br>Your vet will carefully weigh necessity and timing for pregnant patients.</p>
