<p>A sudden food switch is one of the most common, entirely preventable causes of digestive upset in pets — the fix is simple but often skipped.</p>

<h2>Why Gut Bacteria Need Time to Adjust</h2>
<p>Your pet's digestive system adapts to their current diet over time; an abrupt change doesn't give gut bacteria time to adjust accordingly.</p>

<h2>The Gradual Transition Approach</h2>
<p>Mixing increasing proportions of new food with decreasing old food over about a week is a standard, effective transition approach.</p>

<h2>When to Still Watch Closely</h2>
<p>Even gradual transitions can occasionally cause mild upset in sensitive pets — monitor stool and appetite during any diet change.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**How long should a diet transition take?**</strong><br>Roughly 7-10 days is a common general guideline, though sensitive pets may benefit from a longer transition.</p>
<p><strong>**Is this necessary for treats too?**</strong><br>Less critical for occasional treats, but a new regular food component should still be transitioned gradually.</p>
<p><strong>**What if my pet refuses the new food during transition?**</strong><br>Adjust the ratio more slowly, or consult your vet if the refusal persists.</p>
