<p>Fleas and ticks aren't just an itching nuisance — they carry real disease risk, making consistent prevention more valuable than reactive treatment.</p>

<h2>Why Prevention Matters More Than Treatment</h2>
<p>Both fleas and ticks can transmit serious diseases; preventing infestation avoids both the discomfort and the disease risk entirely.</p>

<h2>Signs of Infestation</h2>
<p>Excessive scratching, visible parasites or their debris in the coat, and skin irritation are common signs to watch for.</p>

<h2>Treatment If Already Infested</h2>
<p>Your vet can recommend an appropriate treatment product based on your dog's specific situation and any existing infestation severity.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is year-round prevention really necessary?</strong><br>Yes, in most climates including much of India — pests don't fully disappear seasonally.</p>
<p><strong>Can indoor dogs get fleas and ticks?</strong><br>Yes, exposure can happen through walks, other pets, or even carried in on clothing.</p>
<p><strong>Are over-the-counter products as effective as vet-recommended ones?</strong><br>Vet-recommended products are generally more reliably effective and safer — ask your vet for a specific recommendation.</p>
