<p>The first week after adopting a puppy sets the tone for their long-term health — and vaccination status is usually the first thing to sort out.</p>

<h2>The First Vet Visit</h2>
<p>Schedule a veterinary visit as soon as possible after adoption for a full health check, vaccination history review, and to establish or continue an appropriate vaccination schedule.</p>

<h2>If Vaccination History Is Unknown</h2>
<p>Your vet will typically treat an unknown-history puppy conservatively, starting or restarting an appropriate schedule rather than assuming full protection.</p>

<h2>Beyond Vaccination</h2>
<p>The first visit is also the time to discuss deworming, nutrition, and any immediate health concerns noticed since adoption.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How soon after adoption should the first vet visit happen?</strong><br>As soon as possible, ideally within the first few days.</p>
<p><strong>What if the shelter gave partial vaccination records?</strong><br>Bring these records to your vet, who can build an appropriate plan around them.</p>
<p><strong>Should a new puppy be kept isolated until vaccinated?</strong><br>Generally yes, avoid high-exposure areas like dog parks until your vet confirms adequate protection.</p>
