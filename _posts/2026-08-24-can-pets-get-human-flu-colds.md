<p>It's a reasonable question when someone in the house is sick — but the relationship between human respiratory illness and pet illness is more nuanced than direct transmission.</p>

<h2>The General Answer</h2>
<p>Most common human cold and flu viruses are not typically transmitted to dogs and cats — these species have their own distinct respiratory pathogens.</p>

<h2>Pet-Specific Respiratory Illness</h2>
<p>Dogs and cats have their own respiratory conditions (like kennel cough or cat flu) that aren't the same as human cold or flu viruses.</p>

<h2>When to Still Be Cautious</h2>
<p>If your pet shows respiratory symptoms while a household member is sick, it's more likely coincidental illness or a pet-specific pathogen — evaluate it on its own merits with your vet.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Should I isolate my pet if I have the flu?**</strong><br>Not typically necessary for standard human flu, but general hygiene practices are still reasonable.</p>
<p><strong>**Can my pet catch COVID-19 from me?**</strong><br>Some documented cases exist in various species; ask your vet for current guidance if this is a specific concern.</p>
<p><strong>**Are pet respiratory illnesses contagious to other pets?**</strong><br>Yes, several pet-specific respiratory conditions are contagious between animals, even if not to humans.</p>
