<p>Preventive healthcare works best as a coordinated plan, not a series of disconnected reminders — here's a framework for building one.</p>

<h2>The Core Pillars</h2>
<p>Vaccination, parasite prevention, dental care, nutrition, and regular wellness checkups together form the foundation of a preventive plan.</p>

<h2>Tailoring to Your Specific Pet</h2>
<p>Species, age, lifestyle, and any existing health conditions should all shape which elements get prioritized and how frequently.</p>

<h2>Working With Your Vet as a Partner</h2>
<p>A good preventive plan is built collaboratively, adjusted over time as your pet ages and circumstances change.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Is a generic preventive plan good enough?**</strong><br>A personalized plan, adjusted for your specific pet, tends to serve better than a purely generic checklist.</p>
<p><strong>**How often should the plan be reviewed?**</strong><br>At least annually, more often for senior pets or those with ongoing conditions.</p>
<p><strong>**Does preventive care really save money long-term?**</strong><br>Often yes, since catching issues early tends to be less costly than treating advanced disease.</p>
