<p>Beyond the well-known examples like rabies, several other zoonotic risks are worth understanding as a pet owner, even if the actual risk is generally low with good care.</p>

<h2>Parasites</h2>
<p>Certain intestinal parasites can transmit to humans, particularly children, making regular deworming an important preventive measure.</p>

<h2>Fungal Infections</h2>
<p>Ringworm is a common zoonotic fungal infection that spreads relatively easily between pets and humans in close contact.</p>

<h2>Reducing Overall Risk</h2>
<p>Consistent veterinary preventive care, good hygiene, and prompt attention to any pet illness together form the core of practical risk reduction.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Should pregnant women avoid pets due to zoonotic risk?**</strong><br>Certain specific precautions (like litter box handling) are commonly recommended — discuss specifics with a doctor and vet.</p>
<p><strong>**Can indoor pets still pose zoonotic risk?**</strong><br>Yes, though generally lower than pets with significant outdoor exposure.</p>
<p><strong>**Is regular vet care the main defense against zoonotic risk?**</strong><br>Yes, it's the most effective, practical approach available to pet owners.</p>
