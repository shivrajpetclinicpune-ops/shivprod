<p>Cats are particularly sensitive to the stress of transport — getting this right matters both for their comfort and for an accurate exam once you arrive.</p>

<h2>Use a Secure Carrier</h2>
<p>A proper, secure carrier — not just holding your cat — reduces injury risk and keeps them from escaping in a moment of panic.</p>

<h2>Reduce Stress</h2>
<p>Covering the carrier partially with a light cloth, keeping the car quiet, and minimizing handling can all help reduce anxiety during the trip.</p>

<h2>For a Seriously Ill or Injured Cat</h2>
<p>Handle gently, avoid unnecessary movement, and keep them as calm and warm as possible en route.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Should I familiarize my cat with the carrier beforehand?</strong><br>Yes, this can meaningfully reduce stress for routine visits — though in an emergency, use whatever secure option is available.</p>
<p><strong>Is it safe to sedate my cat myself for transport?</strong><br>No, don't administer any medication without direct veterinary guidance.</p>
<p><strong>Should I keep the carrier in view during the car ride?</strong><br>Placing it somewhere stable and not sliding around is more important than visibility for your cat's comfort.</p>
