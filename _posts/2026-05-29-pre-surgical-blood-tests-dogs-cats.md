<p>Pre-surgical blood testing is a routine but genuinely important step — here's what pet parents should understand before a scheduled procedure.</p>

<h2>What's Typically Tested</h2>
<p>A CBC and biochemistry panel are commonly run to screen for underlying issues that could affect anesthesia and surgery.</p>

<h2>Timing</h2>
<p>This is usually done shortly before the scheduled procedure, sometimes the same day, to reflect the pet's current condition.</p>

<h2>What If Results Are Abnormal</h2>
<p>Your vet may adjust the anesthetic plan, recommend further testing, or in some cases postpone the procedure based on findings.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is this test mandatory for every pet before surgery?</strong><br>Strongly recommended for most procedures, particularly for older pets or those with any health history.</p>
<p><strong>Does fasting affect these results?</strong><br>Your vet will provide specific pre-test instructions, including any fasting requirements.</p>
<p><strong>Can I request this test even if not specifically recommended?</strong><br>Yes, discuss this option with your vet if you'd like additional peace of mind.</p>
