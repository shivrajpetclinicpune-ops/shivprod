<h2>Can Heat Be Controlled Without Surgery?</h2>
<p>Yes — but only temporarily, and always under veterinary supervision. There is no safe, effective way to permanently manage a cat's reproductive cycle without eventually addressing it surgically or medically through proper veterinary channels.</p>

<h2>Non-Surgical Options</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Hormonal medications</strong> — short-term use only, prescribed and monitored by a vet; not intended for repeated or long-term use</li>
<li><strong>GnRH implants</strong> — availability varies by region and product; these suppress the cycle for a defined period under veterinary guidance</li>
<li><strong>Environmental management</strong> — keeping a cat strictly indoors and away from intact males reduces unwanted breeding risk, though it does not stop the heat cycle itself</li>
</ul>

<div class="post-highlight">🔍 Clinical insight: At Shivraj Pet Clinic, the cats we see most often in emergency for pyometra (a life-threatening uterine infection) have a history of repeated hormonal suppression rather than spaying. The short-term convenience carries a real long-term cost.</div>

<h2>What to Avoid</h2>
<p>❌ Human hormone tablets • Herbal remedies of unproven safety • Sedatives used to "calm" heat behaviour • Alcohol or other home remedies circulating online</p>

<div class="post-warning">⚠️ Never administer human medication, sedatives, or unverified herbal products to suppress your cat's heat. These are not evaluated for feline safety and can cause serious harm.</div>

<h2>Why Spaying Is Still the Best Long-Term Option</h2>
<p>Spaying permanently prevents unwanted pregnancies and significantly reduces the risk of pyometra and mammary tumours — risks that increase with every unmanaged heat cycle. It also eliminates the behavioural stress of repeated heat cycles for both the cat and the household.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is it safe to use hormonal medication just once?</strong><br>A single, vet-supervised use for a specific short-term situation carries far less risk than repeated or ongoing use — but it should always be discussed with your vet first.</p>
<p><strong>At what age can I spay my cat?</strong><br>We generally recommend spaying by 5–6 months, ideally before the first heat cycle.</p>
<p><strong>Does letting a cat go through heat without mating cause harm?</strong><br>Repeated unmanaged cycles over a long period do increase reproductive health risks over time, even without mating.</p>
<p><strong>Can indoor-only cats still go into heat?</strong><br>Yes — heat cycles occur regardless of indoor or outdoor lifestyle, though indoor cats have a much lower unwanted pregnancy risk.</p>
<p><strong>Are GnRH implants available in Pune?</strong><br>Availability varies — ask us during a consultation about current options.</p>
<p><strong>What are the signs my cat needs emergency care during heat management?</strong><br>Lethargy, loss of appetite, vomiting, or abdominal swelling need immediate attention, particularly in cats with a history of hormonal suppression.</p>
<p><strong>Is spaying safe for an older cat?</strong><br>Yes, with proper pre-surgical assessment — age alone isn't a barrier, though risk factors are evaluated individually.</p>
<p><strong>How soon after using hormonal medication can I schedule spaying?</strong><br>This depends on the specific medication used — we'll advise timing during your consultation.</p>
