<p>Kidney disease is common enough in senior cats that recognizing the early signs — often subtle — can meaningfully change how the condition is managed.</p>

<h2>Early Signs</h2>
<p>Increased thirst and urination, mild weight loss, and reduced appetite are often among the earliest, subtlest indicators.</p>

<h2>Why Early Detection Matters</h2>
<p>Kidney disease is generally progressive, and earlier detection allows management strategies to potentially slow progression more effectively.</p>

<h2>Diagnosis</h2>
<p>Bloodwork and urine testing together provide the clearest picture of kidney function and disease stage.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Can kidney disease be cured?**</strong><br>Generally managed rather than cured, though management can significantly support quality of life.</p>
<p><strong>**Is diet important for cats with kidney disease?**</strong><br>Yes, specific dietary management is often a key part of the treatment approach.</p>
<p><strong>**How often should senior cats be screened?**</strong><br>Often recommended every six months as part of senior wellness monitoring.</p>
