<p>Cats have some distinct disease tendencies compared to dogs, which shapes which tests get prioritized when a cat is unwell.</p>

<h2>Starting Point</h2>
<p>CBC and biochemistry panels remain the common starting point, similar to dogs, providing a broad initial picture.</p>

<h2>Cat-Specific Considerations</h2>
<p>Given the prevalence of kidney disease and hyperthyroidism in cats, these areas often receive particular attention in senior or symptomatic cats.</p>

<h2>Why Individualization Matters</h2>
<p>A young cat with acute vomiting and a senior cat with weight loss will likely need different test emphasis based on likely underlying causes.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is thyroid testing routine for all sick cats?</strong><br>It's commonly included for older cats or those with relevant symptoms like weight loss with increased appetite.</p>
<p><strong>How is testing different for kittens?</strong><br>Kittens may need different reference ranges and additional consideration for infectious disease given their developing immune systems.</p>
<p><strong>Can urine testing complement blood work in cats?</strong><br>Yes, particularly for evaluating kidney function and urinary issues.</p>
