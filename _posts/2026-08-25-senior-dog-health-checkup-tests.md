<p>Senior dogs face a different risk profile than younger adults, which is why their checkups are typically more comprehensive, not just more frequent.</p>

<h2>Why Senior Checkups Differ</h2>
<p>Age-related conditions — kidney disease, arthritis, heart disease, cognitive changes — become more likely, warranting closer monitoring.</p>

<h2>Common Components</h2>
<p>Bloodwork, blood pressure assessment, joint and mobility evaluation, and dental checks are common additions to a senior wellness visit.</p>

<h2>Frequency</h2>
<p>Many vets recommend senior checkups every six months rather than annually, given how quickly some conditions can progress.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**What age counts as senior for dogs?**</strong><br>This varies by breed size — larger breeds are often considered senior earlier than smaller breeds.</p>
<p><strong>**Is bloodwork always included in senior checkups?**</strong><br>Commonly recommended, though your vet will advise based on your dog's specific history.</p>
<p><strong>**Can early detection meaningfully change outcomes?**</strong><br>Yes, for several common senior conditions, earlier detection generally supports better management.</p>
