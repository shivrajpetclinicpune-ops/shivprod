<p>Indian summers make heatstroke a real, common, and dangerous risk for dogs — particularly flat-faced breeds, seniors, and dogs left in parked cars or without shade. Minutes matter here more than in almost any other emergency.</p>

<h2>Early Warning Signs</h2>
<p>Heavy panting that doesn't settle, bright red gums, excessive drooling, weakness, and disorientation are early signs. As it progresses, vomiting, collapse, and seizures can follow.</p>

<h2>Immediate First Aid</h2>
<p>Move your dog to a cool, shaded area immediately. Apply cool (not ice-cold) water to the body, particularly the belly, paws, and neck, and use a fan if available. Offer small amounts of cool water to drink if your dog is alert enough to drink safely. Do not use ice-cold water or ice directly on the skin — this can constrict blood vessels and actually slow cooling.</p>

<h2>Get to a Vet Immediately, Even If Cooling Seems to Help</h2>
<p>Heatstroke causes internal damage that isn't visible from the outside — even a dog that seems to recover with initial cooling needs veterinary evaluation for organ function, clotting, and hydration status.</p>

<h2>Prevention</h2>
<p>Never leave a dog in a parked car, even briefly, even with windows cracked. Walk during cooler hours, provide constant access to shade and water, and watch flat-faced breeds (Pugs, Bulldogs) and senior dogs especially closely, since they overheat faster.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How quickly can heatstroke become dangerous?</strong><br>Very quickly — it can escalate from early signs to a life-threatening state within minutes to an hour, which is why immediate action matters more than in most conditions.</p>
<p><strong>Is it safe to use ice on my dog to cool them faster?</strong><br>No — ice or ice-cold water can be counterproductive by constricting blood vessels near the skin. Use cool, not ice-cold, water instead.</p>
<p><strong>Which breeds are most at risk?</strong><br>Flat-faced (brachycephalic) breeds like Pugs and Bulldogs, along with senior dogs, overweight dogs, and thick-coated breeds, are all at elevated risk.</p>
<p><strong>My dog seems fine after I cooled them down — do I still need a vet visit?</strong><br>Yes — internal effects of heatstroke aren't visible externally and need to be checked.</p>
