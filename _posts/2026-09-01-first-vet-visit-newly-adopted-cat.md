<p>An adopted cat's first vet visit establishes the health foundation for their new life with you — here's what that visit typically involves.</p>

<h2>What to Bring</h2>
<p>Any available shelter or previous owner records, and notes on behavior or symptoms observed since adoption.</p>

<h2>What the Visit Typically Covers</h2>
<p>A full physical exam, vaccination and deworming status review, and often baseline bloodwork given the frequency of unknown history in adopted cats.</p>

<h2>Settling-In Considerations</h2>
<p>Your vet can also advise on helping a newly adopted cat adjust, particularly around stress, litter box habits, and introducing them to a new home safely.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Should a newly adopted cat be tested for infectious diseases?**</strong><br>Often recommended given unknown history — ask your vet about relevant screening.</p>
<p><strong>**How soon should the first visit happen after adoption?**</strong><br>As soon as possible, ideally within the first few days.</p>
<p><strong>**Is stress-related illness common after adoption?**</strong><br>Yes, the transition can be stressful for cats — mention any behavior changes to your vet.</p>
