<p>Beak and nail overgrowth in parrots is sometimes normal wear-and-tear related, but can also be the visible sign of a metabolic or liver issue underneath.</p>

<h2>Normal Wear vs Overgrowth</h2>
<p>Regular access to appropriate perches and chew toys typically keeps beak and nails naturally worn — overgrowth beyond this can indicate insufficient wear opportunities or an underlying issue.</p>

<h2>When Overgrowth Signals Illness</h2>
<p>Rapid or unusual beak overgrowth can sometimes be linked to liver disease or other metabolic conditions, not just insufficient wear.</p>

<h2>Why Trimming Needs Professional Care</h2>
<p>Incorrect trimming risks pain, bleeding, and injury — this isn't a safe home procedure without proper training.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can I trim my parrot's nails at home?</strong><br>This carries injury risk without proper training — professional trimming is safer, especially initially.</p>
<p><strong>Does beak overgrowth always mean illness?</strong><br>Not always, but persistent or rapid overgrowth should be evaluated to rule out underlying causes.</p>
<p><strong>How often do beaks typically need trimming?</strong><br>This varies by individual bird and available wear opportunities — ask your avian vet for a personalized schedule.</p>
