<p>Proximity is convenient, but it shouldn't be the only factor in choosing where your pet gets care — a few other things matter more.</p>

<h2>Range of Services</h2>
<p>Does the clinic offer in-house diagnostics, handle emergencies, and treat the specific species you own (including exotics, if relevant)?</p>

<h2>Communication and Transparency</h2>
<p>A good clinic explains findings and treatment options clearly, and is upfront about costs before proceeding.</p>

<h2>Experience and Facilities</h2>
<p>Ask about the vet's experience with your pet's specific needs and the clinic's diagnostic and treatment capabilities.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is proximity still worth considering?</strong><br>Yes, especially for emergencies — a balance of quality and reasonable distance is ideal.</p>
<p><strong>Should I ask about emergency availability upfront?</strong><br>Yes, this is worth clarifying before you need it, not during a crisis.</p>
<p><strong>Can I switch clinics if unsatisfied?</strong><br>Yes, and requesting your pet's records for continuity is a reasonable, common practice.</p>
