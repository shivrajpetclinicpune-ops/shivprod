<p>The liver has significant functional reserve, which means liver disease can be quite advanced before obvious symptoms appear — making diagnostic screening valuable.</p>

<h2>Possible Signs</h2>
<p>Reduced appetite, lethargy, vomiting, changes in thirst, and in more advanced cases, yellowing of the gums or eyes (jaundice).</p>

<h2>Why Symptoms Can Be Subtle Early On</h2>
<p>The liver's functional reserve means significant damage can occur before clinical signs become obvious to an owner.</p>

<h2>Diagnosis</h2>
<p>Blood biochemistry testing is typically the first step, sometimes followed by imaging or further specific testing based on findings.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Can liver disease be caused by diet?**</strong><br>Certain dietary factors can contribute, alongside other causes like toxins, infections, and age-related changes.</p>
<p><strong>**Is jaundice always present with liver disease?**</strong><br>No, it's a sign of more advanced disease, not present in all or early cases.</p>
<p><strong>**Can liver disease be managed long-term?**</strong><br>Depending on the cause and severity, many cases can be managed with appropriate veterinary care and diet.</p>
