<p>Rabbits breathe almost exclusively through their nose, which means nasal or respiratory issues affect them more seriously than in species that can easily switch to mouth breathing.</p>

<h2>Warning Signs</h2>
<p>Nasal discharge, sneezing, labored breathing, noisy breathing, and reduced activity can all indicate a respiratory problem.</p>

<h2>Why This Is Higher Risk in Rabbits</h2>
<p>Because rabbits rely on nasal breathing, any nasal obstruction or infection has an outsized impact on their ability to breathe comfortably.</p>

<h2>When to Seek Emergency Care</h2>
<p>Open-mouth breathing, significant nasal discharge, or visible breathing effort should be treated as urgent.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can rabbits mouth-breathe if needed?</strong><br>It's not their normal pattern and open-mouth breathing signals real distress.</p>
<p><strong>Are certain rabbit breeds more prone to breathing issues?</strong><br>Some flat-faced breeds have higher risk due to airway anatomy.</p>
<p><strong>Should nasal discharge always be checked?</strong><br>Yes, it can indicate infection or other underlying problems.</p>
