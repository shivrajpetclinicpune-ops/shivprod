<p>Home observation has real limits when it comes to dental health — much of what matters is only visible or assessable during a proper veterinary exam.</p>

<h2>What a Dental Check Catches Early</h2>
<p>Tartar buildup, early gum disease, and subtle changes that aren't visible or obvious from casual home observation.</p>

<h2>Why Early Detection Matters</h2>
<p>Early dental disease is significantly easier and less invasive to address than advanced disease, which can require more extensive treatment.</p>

<h2>How Often</h2>
<p>Typically part of routine wellness visits, with your vet advising more frequent checks if early findings warrant closer monitoring.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Can I assess dental health myself at home?**</strong><br>You can spot obvious signs, but a full assessment, including under the gumline, needs a proper veterinary exam.</p>
<p><strong>**Does every pet need a professional dental cleaning eventually?**</strong><br>Many do, particularly as they age — your vet can advise based on individual dental health.</p>
<p><strong>**Is anesthesia required for a proper dental cleaning?**</strong><br>Typically yes, for a thorough and safe cleaning, including below the gumline.</p>
