<p>A dog that suddenly can't get up is one of the more frightening things an owner can witness — and rightly so, because the list of possible causes ranges widely in urgency.</p>

<h2>Possible Causes</h2>
<p>Sudden weakness can stem from low blood sugar, anemia, internal bleeding, heart problems, neurological issues, spinal injury, poisoning, or severe pain. The wide range is exactly why this symptom needs a professional assessment rather than a guess.</p>

<h2>What to Check Immediately</h2>
<p>Look at gum color (pale or white gums suggest anemia or shock), check for obvious injury, and note whether your dog is alert and responsive or dazed. Don't force your dog to walk or stand if there's any suspicion of spinal injury.</p>

<h2>Moving a Weak or Injured Dog Safely</h2>
<p>If a spinal injury is possible, use a flat, firm surface as a makeshift stretcher rather than carrying your dog under the belly. Keep movement minimal and get to the clinic promptly.</p>

<h2>Why This Is Always Treated as Urgent</h2>
<p>Even when the cause turns out to be minor, sudden weakness or an inability to stand should be treated as an emergency until proven otherwise — the downside of waiting is far worse than the inconvenience of an unnecessary visit.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Could this just be old age catching up?</strong><br>Age-related joint or muscle weakness usually develops gradually, not suddenly — a sudden onset points toward an acute cause that needs evaluation.</p>
<p><strong>My dog collapsed briefly then seemed fine — do I still need to go in?</strong><br>Yes. A brief collapse can be caused by heart, neurological, or metabolic issues that won't necessarily show again immediately but still need investigating.</p>
<p><strong>Can low blood sugar cause this in adult dogs, not just puppies?</strong><br>Yes, though it's more common in puppies and small-breed dogs — certain medical conditions can cause it in adults too.</p>
<p><strong>What tests help find the cause?</strong><br>Bloodwork, a neurological exam, and sometimes imaging (X-ray or ultrasound) are typically used to narrow down the cause.</p>
