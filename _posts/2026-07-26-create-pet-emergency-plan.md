<p>The best time to plan for a pet emergency is before it happens — a few simple preparations can save critical time later.</p>

<h2>Save Key Contacts</h2>
<p>Your regular vet and a backup emergency clinic's contact information, saved in your phone where you can find it instantly under stress.</p>

<h2>Know the Fastest Route</h2>
<p>Have a general sense of how to get to your emergency option quickly, particularly if it's not your regular clinic.</p>

<h2>Keep Records Accessible</h2>
<p>Vaccination history and any known medical conditions, stored somewhere you can quickly reference or share.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Should every household member know the emergency plan?</strong><br>Yes, ideally everyone who might need to act knows where to go and who to call.</p>
<p><strong>Is a first-aid kit part of this plan?</strong><br>Yes, it's a complementary piece — see our pet first-aid kit article for specifics.</p>
<p><strong>How often should I review this plan?</strong><br>Periodically, and especially if you move or change vets.</p>
