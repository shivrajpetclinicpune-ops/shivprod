<p>Deworming isn't a one-time task — it's a schedule, and kittens in particular need it started early and repeated on a specific timeline because of how easily they pick up parasites.</p>

<h2>Why Kittens Are High-Risk</h2>
<p>Kittens can be born with parasites transmitted from the mother, or pick them up quickly through nursing or their environment. Left untreated, parasites can cause poor growth, a pot-bellied appearance, diarrhea, and weakness.</p>

<h2>General Schedule Pattern</h2>
<p>Deworming for kittens typically starts around 2-3 weeks of age and repeats every 2-3 weeks until around 12 weeks, then transitions to a regular adult schedule. Your vet will set the exact intervals based on your kitten's situation.</p>

<h2>Signs of a Parasite Burden</h2>
<p>A pot-bellied appearance despite otherwise normal or thin body condition, poor growth, visible worms or segments in stool, diarrhea, and a dull coat can all indicate a parasite burden worth addressing promptly.</p>

<h2>Why the Schedule Matters More Than a Single Dose</h2>
<p>A single deworming dose doesn't cover parasites at every life stage present in the kitten's system — the repeated schedule is designed to catch parasites as they cycle through, which a one-off treatment misses.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can kittens be dewormed before they show any symptoms?</strong><br>Yes — deworming is typically done on a preventive schedule regardless of visible symptoms, since parasites are extremely common in kittens.</p>
<p><strong>Is deworming safe for very young kittens?</strong><br>Yes, when using age-appropriate products and dosing under veterinary guidance.</p>
<p><strong>Can humans catch parasites from kittens?</strong><br>Some parasites are zoonotic (transmissible to humans), which is another reason to stay on schedule and practice good hygiene, especially with children in the home.</p>
<p><strong>When does a kitten move to adult deworming frequency?</strong><br>Typically after completing the kitten series around 12 weeks, transitioning to a regular adult schedule your vet will outline.</p>
