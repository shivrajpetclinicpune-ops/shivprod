<p>It's easy to think of fleas as just an itchy nuisance, but a heavy infestation can cause genuine, dangerous blood loss — particularly in small or young pets.</p>

<h2>How This Happens</h2>
<p>Fleas feed on blood, and a severe infestation, especially relative to a small body size, can cause significant cumulative blood loss over time.</p>

<h2>Highest-Risk Pets</h2>
<p>Puppies, kittens, and small or already-debilitated animals are at the highest risk of flea-related anemia due to their smaller blood volume.</p>

<h2>Signs of Flea-Related Anemia</h2>
<p>Pale gums, weakness, and lethargy alongside visible flea infestation should prompt urgent veterinary attention.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>How severe does an infestation need to be to cause anemia?</strong><br>This varies by pet size, but heavy, prolonged infestations pose the greatest risk, especially in small animals.</p>
<p><strong>Is this an emergency?</strong><br>Yes, if anemia signs (pale gums, weakness) are present alongside a flea infestation.</p>
<p><strong>Can prevention entirely eliminate this risk?</strong><br>Consistent flea prevention significantly reduces the risk of ever reaching this severity.</p>
