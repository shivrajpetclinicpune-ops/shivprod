<p>Some symptoms are universal red flags across species — here's a quick-reference list worth keeping in mind.</p>

<h2>The List</h2>
<p>Difficulty breathing, collapse, severe bleeding, repeated vomiting or seizures, inability to urinate, suspected poisoning, a bloated abdomen, sudden weakness, severe pain, and extreme lethargy.</p>

<h2>Why These Specifically</h2>
<p>Each of these can indicate a rapidly progressing, potentially life-threatening condition where delay meaningfully worsens outcomes.</p>

<h2>What to Do</h2>
<p>Any of these signs warrants an immediate call to your vet or emergency clinic — not a wait-and-watch approach.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Does one symptom always mean the worst-case scenario?</strong><br>Not necessarily, but these signs warrant urgent evaluation to determine the actual cause and appropriate treatment.</p>
<p><strong>Should I keep this list somewhere accessible?</strong><br>Yes, having a quick mental or physical reference can help you act decisively when it matters.</p>
<p><strong>Are these signs the same across all pet species?</strong><br>Largely similar principles apply, though presentation can vary somewhat by species.</p>
