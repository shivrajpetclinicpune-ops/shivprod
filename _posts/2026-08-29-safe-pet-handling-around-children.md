<p>Most pet bites and scratches involving children are preventable with the right household habits and age-appropriate supervision.</p>

<h2>Supervision Matters</h2>
<p>Direct supervision during interactions, particularly with young children and any pet, significantly reduces risk of an unwanted incident.</p>

<h2>Teaching Respectful Interaction</h2>
<p>Teaching children to recognize a pet's body language signals — and to give pets space when eating, sleeping, or showing signs of stress — builds safer habits.</p>

<h2>Setting Up Safe Spaces</h2>
<p>Ensuring your pet has an accessible retreat space where they won't be disturbed reduces stress-driven defensive reactions.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**At what age can children safely interact with pets unsupervised?**</strong><br>This varies by child maturity and pet temperament — direct supervision is generally recommended for young children regardless.</p>
<p><strong>**Are certain pet behaviors warning signs to teach children?**</strong><br>Yes, teaching children to recognize signs like growling, stiffening, or trying to move away as signals to give space is valuable.</p>
<p><strong>**Should new pets be introduced carefully to children?**</strong><br>Yes, gradual, supervised introductions generally work better than immediate, unsupervised interaction.</p>
