<p>The case for regular bloodwork gets stronger with age — several conditions common in seniors develop silently and are best caught through testing, not observation.</p>

<h2>Why Age Changes the Calculation</h2>
<p>Kidney disease, liver changes, diabetes, and thyroid conditions all become more common with age, and many produce few early visible symptoms.</p>

<h2>What Regular Testing Catches</h2>
<p>Trends over time — not just a single result — can reveal gradual changes that a single visit's snapshot might miss.</p>

<h2>How Often</h2>
<p>Many vets recommend senior bloodwork every six months, though your vet can advise based on your pet's specific health history.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Is this necessary even for a senior pet that seems healthy?**</strong><br>Yes, several conditions produce minimal visible symptoms in early stages.</p>
<p><strong>**Does tracking trends really matter more than a single result?**</strong><br>Yes, gradual changes over time can be meaningful even when each individual result looks only mildly abnormal.</p>
<p><strong>**Should I request this even if my vet doesn't bring it up?**</strong><br>Yes, feel free to ask about senior wellness testing directly if it hasn't been discussed.</p>
