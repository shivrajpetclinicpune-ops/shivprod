<p>How you move a sick dog to the clinic can matter as much as how quickly you get there — especially if injury or severe weakness is involved.</p>

<h2>For a Weak but Mobile Dog</h2>
<p>Support their body gently, avoid unnecessary handling of a painful area, and keep them calm during the car ride.</p>

<h2>For a Dog That Can't Walk or Has Suspected Spinal Injury</h2>
<p>Use a flat, firm surface (like a board) as a makeshift stretcher rather than carrying by the belly, and minimize movement.</p>

<h2>General Tips</h2>
<p>Call ahead if possible so the clinic can prepare, and keep the car ride as calm and stable as you can manage.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Should I muzzle an injured, painful dog before moving them?</strong><br>A painful, frightened dog can bite unexpectedly — a muzzle can be a safety measure if your dog will tolerate it, but don't delay transport significantly to apply one.</p>
<p><strong>Is it safe to give my dog water during transport?</strong><br>Small amounts are often fine for a conscious, alert dog, but avoid forcing anything if they seem unable to swallow safely.</p>
<p><strong>Should someone else drive while I monitor my dog?</strong><br>This is ideal if available, allowing you to keep your dog calm and monitored during the trip.</p>
