<p>Appetite loss in a newly adopted young pet can stem from stress alone — but given how quickly puppies and kittens can decline, it still needs prompt attention.</p>

<h2>Why This Happens</h2>
<p>Stress from a new environment, unfamiliar food, and separation from previous surroundings can all temporarily suppress appetite in newly adopted young pets.</p>

<h2>Why It's Still Urgent in This Age Group</h2>
<p>Puppies and kittens have limited energy reserves and can weaken quickly — even stress-related appetite loss needs a shorter monitoring window than in adults.</p>

<h2>What to Do</h2>
<p>If appetite doesn't improve within several hours to a day, or if any other symptoms appear, contact your vet rather than continuing to wait.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Is stress-related appetite loss common after adoption?**</strong><br>Yes, it's a common, usually short-lived response to a major environment change.</p>
<p><strong>**Should I try the previous food if known?**</strong><br>Yes, matching previous diet initially, then transitioning gradually, can help reduce stress-related refusal.</p>
<p><strong>**How long is genuinely safe to wait before calling the vet?**</strong><br>Given the fast decline risk in young pets, don't wait beyond about a day without checking in with your vet.</p>
