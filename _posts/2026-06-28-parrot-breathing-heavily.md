<p>A bird's respiratory system leaves little room for error — heavy breathing that would be minor in a mammal can indicate a serious problem in a parrot.</p>

<h2>Warning Signs</h2>
<p>Tail bobbing with each breath, open-mouth breathing, wheezing, and audible clicking sounds are significant warning signs.</p>

<h2>Possible Causes</h2>
<p>Respiratory infection, nutritional deficiency affecting the respiratory tract, or exposure to fumes and toxins can all cause breathing difficulty.</p>

<h2>Why This Is Always an Emergency</h2>
<p>Given how quickly birds can deteriorate respiratorily, any labored breathing should be treated as urgent, not observed at home.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can fumes from cooking cause this?</strong><br>Yes, birds are highly sensitive to airborne toxins, including certain non-stick cookware fumes.</p>
<p><strong>Is tail bobbing always serious?</strong><br>It's a recognized sign of respiratory effort and should prompt an urgent vet visit.</p>
<p><strong>Can this resolve without treatment?</strong><br>Don't assume so — get an urgent evaluation.</p>
