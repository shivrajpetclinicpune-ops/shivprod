<p>Diarrhea is common enough that it's tempting to dismiss — but the difference between a one-day upset and a serious problem often comes down to a few specific warning signs, not just how it looks.</p>

<h2>Mild, Self-Limiting Diarrhea</h2>
<p>A single episode of soft stool in a dog that's otherwise bright, eating normally, and not showing other symptoms is often caused by a minor dietary indiscretion or stress, and frequently resolves within a day with a bland diet.</p>

<div class="post-highlight">💡 Diarrhea lasting more than 24-48 hours, blood in the stool, black or tarry stool, diarrhea combined with vomiting or lethargy, or diarrhea in a puppy or senior dog all warrant veterinary evaluation rather than home management.</div>

<h2>Why Puppies and Seniors Are Different</h2>
<p>Both groups dehydrate faster and have less physiological reserve than a healthy adult dog. What might be a manageable day-long upset in a healthy adult can become dangerous much faster in a young puppy or an older dog with other health conditions.</p>

<h2>What Might Be Behind Persistent Diarrhea</h2>
<p>Causes range from dietary indiscretion and parasites to infections, food intolerance, and — in more serious cases — gastroenteritis or systemic illness. Persistent or worsening diarrhea usually needs diagnostic workup, not just a diet change, to identify the actual cause.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Should I withhold food if my dog has diarrhea?</strong><br>A short period of a bland, easily digestible diet is often recommended, but check with your vet rather than fasting for an extended period, especially in small or young dogs.</p>
<p><strong>Is blood in the stool always serious?</strong><br>It should always be evaluated — it can range from mild irritation to something requiring urgent treatment, and it's not something to diagnose from home.</p>
<p><strong>Can stress cause diarrhea in dogs?</strong><br>Yes — travel, boarding, or a change in routine are common, usually mild and short-lived triggers.</p>
<p><strong>When does diarrhea count as an emergency versus a routine visit?</strong><br>Diarrhea alongside vomiting, lethargy, blood, or in a very young or senior dog should be treated with more urgency than a same-day-improving case in an otherwise healthy adult.</p>
