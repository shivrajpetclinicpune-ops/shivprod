<p>Biochemistry testing looks beyond blood cells to organ function and metabolic markers — a different, complementary window into your pet's health compared to a CBC.</p>

<h2>What's Measured</h2>
<p>Kidney and liver-related values, blood glucose, proteins, and electrolytes are common components of a standard biochemistry panel.</p>

<h2>Why It Matters</h2>
<p>Many organ conditions produce few visible symptoms early on — biochemistry testing can flag changes before they become obvious clinically.</p>

<h2>Interpreting Results</h2>
<p>Abnormal values don't point to one single diagnosis on their own; they're interpreted alongside history, exam findings, and sometimes further testing.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is fasting required before this test?</strong><br>Sometimes recommended depending on the panel — your vet will advise.</p>
<p><strong>Can this test detect diabetes?</strong><br>Yes, blood glucose is a standard component that can indicate diabetes.</p>
<p><strong>How often should senior pets have this test?</strong><br>Often recommended annually or more frequently as part of senior wellness monitoring.</p>
