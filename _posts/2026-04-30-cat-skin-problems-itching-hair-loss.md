<p>Cat skin problems often show up differently than in dogs — over-grooming in particular is a distinctly feline pattern worth knowing about.</p>

<h2>Common Causes</h2>
<p>Allergies, fleas, ringworm, and stress-related over-grooming are common causes of skin issues in cats.</p>

<h2>Over-Grooming as a Sign</h2>
<p>Cats often respond to skin irritation, or even stress, by over-grooming a specific area, leading to hair loss that can look different from other causes.</p>

<h2>When to See a Vet</h2>
<p>Persistent itching, visible skin lesions, or noticeable hair loss patterns warrant a veterinary evaluation.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can stress alone cause visible hair loss in cats?</strong><br>Yes, psychogenic over-grooming is a recognized cause in some cats.</p>
<p><strong>Is ringworm contagious to humans?</strong><br>Yes, it's a zoonotic fungal infection — hygiene precautions matter if suspected.</p>
<p><strong>Are indoor cats less likely to have skin problems?</strong><br>Somewhat lower risk for parasite-related causes, but allergies and stress-related issues can still occur.</p>
