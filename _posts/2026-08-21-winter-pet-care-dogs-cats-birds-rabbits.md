<p>Winter care needs vary meaningfully by species — what protects a dog's paws isn't the same consideration as what protects a bird's body heat.</p>

<h2>Dogs and Cats</h2>
<p>Provide adequate warmth, particularly for short-haired breeds, seniors, and very young pets, and monitor for signs of discomfort in cold conditions.</p>

<h2>Birds</h2>
<p>Birds are sensitive to temperature drops and drafts — maintaining a stable, appropriately warm environment matters more in winter.</p>

<h2>Rabbits</h2>
<p>Outdoor or semi-outdoor rabbits need protection from cold drafts and adequate bedding; monitor for reduced activity that could signal discomfort or illness.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Do all pets need extra warmth in winter?**</strong><br>This varies by species and individual — short-haired, senior, and young pets generally benefit most from extra precautions.</p>
<p><strong>**Can cold weather worsen arthritis symptoms?**</strong><br>Yes, cold can exacerbate joint stiffness in pets with arthritis — appropriate warmth and gentle activity help.</p>
<p><strong>**Should feeding change in winter?**</strong><br>Some pets may need slight caloric adjustment based on activity changes — ask your vet if unsure.</p>
