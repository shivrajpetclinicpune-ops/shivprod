<p>Knowing what a checkup actually covers helps you get more value from the visit — and know what to flag beforehand.</p>

<h2>Physical Examination</h2>
<p>This typically includes checking the eyes, ears, mouth and teeth, heart and lungs, abdomen, skin and coat, and joints.</p>

<h2>Weight and Body Condition</h2>
<p>Tracking weight trends over time helps catch gradual changes that might otherwise go unnoticed between visits.</p>

<h2>Discussion and Planning</h2>
<p>Your vet will typically discuss vaccination status, parasite prevention, diet, and any concerns or changes you've noticed since the last visit.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Should I prepare anything before the visit?</strong><br>A list of any changes or concerns you've noticed helps make the visit more productive.</p>
<p><strong>Is bloodwork part of a standard checkup?</strong><br>This varies — often recommended for seniors or as clinically indicated, not always included by default.</p>
<p><strong>How long does a typical checkup take?</strong><br>This varies by clinic and pet, but generally ranges from 15-30 minutes for a straightforward visit.</p>
