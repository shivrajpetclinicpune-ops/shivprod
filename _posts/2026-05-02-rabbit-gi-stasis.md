<p>GI stasis — when a rabbit's digestive tract slows or stops moving — is a true emergency and one of the most common reasons rabbits need urgent veterinary care.</p>

<h2>What Causes It</h2>
<p>Low fiber diets, dehydration, stress, pain, dental disease, or an underlying illness can all trigger stasis by disrupting normal gut motility.</p>

<h2>Symptoms</h2>
<p>Reduced or absent droppings, loss of appetite, lethargy, a hunched posture, and teeth grinding from pain are common signs.</p>

<h2>Why It's Urgent</h2>
<p>Once the gut stops moving, gas and bacteria can build up quickly, worsening the rabbit's condition — this needs same-day veterinary intervention, not home monitoring.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can stasis resolve on its own?</strong><br>It's unsafe to assume so — treat any suspected stasis as an emergency.</p>
<p><strong>Is high-fiber diet a prevention strategy?</strong><br>Yes, a hay-based, high-fiber diet significantly reduces stasis risk.</p>
<p><strong>What does treatment involve?</strong><br>Depending on severity, this may include fluids, pain management, and motility support under veterinary care.</p>
