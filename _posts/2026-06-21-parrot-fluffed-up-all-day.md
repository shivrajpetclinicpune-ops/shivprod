<p>Fluffing is a normal thermoregulation behavior — the question is whether it's brief and situational, or constant and combined with other signs.</p>

<h2>Normal Fluffing</h2>
<p>Brief fluffing to stay warm, especially in a cooler room or after bathing, is typically nothing to worry about.</p>

<h2>Concerning Fluffing</h2>
<p>Fluffing that persists all day, combined with lethargy, reduced activity, or appetite changes, points toward illness.</p>

<h2>What to Do</h2>
<p>If fluffing is persistent and paired with any other symptom change, schedule a veterinary visit rather than waiting it out.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is fluffing after a bath normal?</strong><br>Yes, this is typical thermoregulation behavior.</p>
<p><strong>How long is too long for fluffing?</strong><br>Persistent fluffing lasting most of the day, especially with reduced activity, warrants a vet visit.</p>
<p><strong>Can fluffing alone indicate a specific disease?</strong><br>Not specifically — it's a general sign of feeling unwell that needs further evaluation.</p>
