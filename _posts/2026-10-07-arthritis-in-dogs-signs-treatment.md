<p>"He's just getting older" is often the explanation owners give for symptoms that are actually treatable arthritis, not an inevitable part of aging.</p>

<h2>Early Signs</h2>
<p>Stiffness after resting, reluctance to jump or climb stairs, slower to rise, and reduced activity level can all indicate early arthritis.</p>

<h2>Why Early Recognition Matters</h2>
<p>Earlier intervention can improve comfort and slow progression compared to addressing arthritis only once it's significantly advanced.</p>

<h2>Treatment Options</h2>
<p>Management often includes a combination of weight control, appropriate exercise, joint support, and pain management tailored to the individual dog.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Is arthritis only a senior dog condition?**</strong><br>It's more common in seniors but can affect younger dogs too, particularly with joint conditions like hip dysplasia.</p>
<p><strong>**Can weight management really help arthritis?**</strong><br>Yes, significantly — reduced joint load is one of the most effective supportive measures.</p>
<p><strong>**Is arthritis reversible?**</strong><br>Not typically reversible, but well managed with appropriate treatment to maintain comfort and mobility.</p>
