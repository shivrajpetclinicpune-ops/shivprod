<p>Senior cats face a fairly predictable set of common conditions — knowing them helps you and your vet watch proactively rather than reactively.</p>

<h2>Common Conditions</h2>
<p>Kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, dental disease, and arthritis are among the most common conditions seen in senior cats.</p>

<h2>Why Cats Mask These Well</h2>
<p>Cats' tendency to hide illness means these conditions often progress further before becoming visibly obvious than they would in a more expressive species.</p>

<h2>The Value of Regular Monitoring</h2>
<p>Regular senior wellness checks, including bloodwork, catch these conditions earlier than waiting for obvious symptoms.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**At what age is a cat considered senior?**</strong><br>Generally around 7 years and older, though this can vary.</p>
<p><strong>**Is weight loss always concerning in senior cats?**</strong><br>It's a common early sign of several conditions and should be evaluated rather than dismissed as normal aging.</p>
<p><strong>**How often should senior cats have checkups?**</strong><br>Often recommended every six months given how quickly some conditions can progress.</p>
