<p>Daily brushing is the gold standard, but the realistic answer depends on what you and your dog can actually sustain long-term.</p>

<h2>The Ideal Frequency</h2>
<p>Daily brushing provides the most consistent dental benefit, closely mirroring how effective human dental hygiene works.</p>

<h2>A Realistic Minimum</h2>
<p>Even a few times a week provides meaningful benefit if daily brushing isn't sustainable for your household.</p>

<h2>Building the Habit</h2>
<p>Starting gradually, using dog-safe toothpaste, and building positive associations makes the habit more likely to stick long-term.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Can I use human toothpaste?**</strong><br>No, use a pet-specific toothpaste — human toothpaste can contain ingredients unsafe for dogs.</p>
<p><strong>**What if my dog won't tolerate brushing at all?**</strong><br>Ask your vet about alternative dental support options, like specific dental chews or diets.</p>
<p><strong>**Is it too late to start with an older dog?**</strong><br>No, starting at any age still provides ongoing benefit, though existing dental disease may need addressing first.</p>
