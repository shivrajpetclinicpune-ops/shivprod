<p>Dental disease is remarkably common in dogs — and remarkably preventable with consistent, relatively simple habits.</p>

<h2>Daily Prevention</h2>
<p>Regular tooth brushing with a pet-appropriate toothpaste is the single most effective daily prevention step available.</p>

<h2>Supporting Habits</h2>
<p>Dental chews, appropriate diet, and regular veterinary dental checks all support ongoing oral health between brushings.</p>

<h2>Why Prevention Matters Beyond the Mouth</h2>
<p>Untreated dental disease can affect more than comfort and eating — it's linked to broader systemic health concerns over time.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**How often should I brush my dog's teeth?**</strong><br>Daily is ideal, though even a few times a week provides meaningful benefit.</p>
<p><strong>**Are dental chews a substitute for brushing?**</strong><br>They're a helpful supplement, not a full substitute for regular brushing.</p>
<p><strong>**At what age should dental care start?**</strong><br>Establishing habits early, even with puppies, sets a strong foundation.</p>
