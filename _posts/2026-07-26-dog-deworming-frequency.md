<p>Once past the puppy schedule, deworming frequency for adult dogs isn't one-size-fits-all — lifestyle and exposure risk play a real role.</p>

<h2>General Guidance</h2>
<p>Many vets recommend deworming adult dogs every 3-6 months, though this can vary based on your dog's environment and risk exposure.</p>

<h2>Higher-Risk Factors</h2>
<p>Dogs that frequent parks, have contact with other animals, or live in higher-exposure environments may need more frequent deworming.</p>

<h2>Why It's Not Purely Symptom-Based</h2>
<p>Parasites aren't always visibly obvious, so scheduled prevention matters more than waiting for signs to appear.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Do indoor dogs need less frequent deworming?</strong><br>Generally somewhat, but exposure through walks and other pets still applies — ask your vet for a personalized schedule.</p>
<p><strong>Can deworming products vary in what they cover?</strong><br>Yes, different products target different parasite types — your vet can advise the right one.</p>
<p><strong>Is fecal testing useful alongside scheduled deworming?</strong><br>Yes, it can help confirm whether treatment is needed or effective.</p>
