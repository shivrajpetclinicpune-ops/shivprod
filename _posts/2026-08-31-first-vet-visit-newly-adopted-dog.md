<p>An adopted dog's first vet visit often needs to cover more ground than a visit for a dog with known history — here's what to expect.</p>

<h2>What to Bring</h2>
<p>Any records provided by the shelter or previous owner, and a list of behaviors or symptoms you've noticed since adoption.</p>

<h2>What the Visit Typically Covers</h2>
<p>A full physical exam, discussion of vaccination and deworming status (often restarting a schedule if history is unknown), and baseline health assessment.</p>

<h2>Follow-Up Planning</h2>
<p>Your vet will typically outline next steps for vaccination, deworming, and any additional testing based on findings from this first visit.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**What if the shelter says vaccines are up to date but I have no proof?**</strong><br>Discuss this with your vet — they may recommend confirming or restarting certain vaccines depending on the situation.</p>
<p><strong>**Should bloodwork be part of the first visit?**</strong><br>Often recommended, particularly for dogs with unknown health history.</p>
<p><strong>**How soon after adoption should this visit happen?**</strong><br>As soon as possible, ideally within the first few days.</p>
