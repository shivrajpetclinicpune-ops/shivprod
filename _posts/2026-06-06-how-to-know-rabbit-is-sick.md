<p>As a prey species, rabbits instinctively mask illness — which makes knowing the subtle signs more important than waiting for obvious ones.</p>

<h2>Behavioral Signs</h2>
<p>Reduced activity, hiding more than usual, reluctance to move, and reduced interest in food or interaction are early indicators.</p>

<h2>Physical Signs</h2>
<p>Changes in droppings, weight loss, a hunched posture, or a dull coat can all signal illness beneath the surface.</p>

<h2>The Overall Rule</h2>
<p>Any noticeable change from your rabbit's normal routine or behavior — even subtle — is worth a veterinary check rather than a wait-and-see approach.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>What's the fastest way to notice illness early?</strong><br>Knowing your rabbit's normal daily habits closely enough to catch small deviations.</p>
<p><strong>Are rabbits good at hiding pain?</strong><br>Yes, extremely — this is an instinctive survival behavior.</p>
<p><strong>Should any single odd behavior prompt a vet visit?</strong><br>If it persists beyond a few hours or is combined with reduced appetite, yes.</p>
