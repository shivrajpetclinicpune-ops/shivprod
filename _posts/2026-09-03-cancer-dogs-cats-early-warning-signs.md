<p>As pets live longer, cancer has become a more commonly diagnosed condition — early recognition of warning signs genuinely changes treatment options and outcomes.</p>

<h2>Warning Signs</h2>
<p>Unexplained lumps or swellings, non-healing wounds, unexplained weight loss, lethargy, and changes in appetite or bathroom habits can all be relevant signs.</p>

<h2>Why Early Detection Matters So Much Here</h2>
<p>Treatment options and outcomes are often significantly better with earlier-stage detection compared to advanced disease.</p>

<h2>What to Do</h2>
<p>Any new lump, unexplained symptom, or persistent change should be evaluated promptly rather than monitored indefinitely at home.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Are all lumps cancerous?**</strong><br>No, many are benign, but any new lump should still be checked by a vet to determine what it is.</p>
<p><strong>**Is cancer more common in certain breeds?**</strong><br>Yes, some breeds have higher genetic predisposition to specific cancer types.</p>
<p><strong>**Can regular checkups help catch cancer earlier?**</strong><br>Yes, routine exams can catch physical changes before they become obvious to owners at home.</p>
