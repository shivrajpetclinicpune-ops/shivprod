<p>There's no single universal blood test for "sick dog" — the specific panel depends heavily on what symptoms and exam findings are pointing toward.</p>

<h2>Starting Point: CBC and Biochemistry</h2>
<p>These two panels together provide a broad overview and are commonly the starting point for most sick-dog workups.</p>

<h2>Symptom-Specific Additions</h2>
<p>Depending on findings, additional targeted tests may be added — for example, specific organ function tests or infectious disease screening.</p>

<h2>Why It's Individualized</h2>
<p>A vomiting dog and a lethargic senior dog may need overlapping but not identical panels, based on the vet's clinical judgment.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Do all sick dogs need blood tests?</strong><br>Not always — this depends on symptoms, severity, and exam findings.</p>
<p><strong>Can results guide treatment the same day?</strong><br>Same-visit results can support faster treatment decisions when available.</p>
<p><strong>Is repeat testing sometimes needed?</strong><br>Yes, particularly for monitoring a hospitalized or seriously ill patient over time.</p>
