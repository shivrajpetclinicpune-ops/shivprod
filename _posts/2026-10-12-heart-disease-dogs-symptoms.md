<p>Heart disease in dogs often develops gradually, which means early symptoms can be easy to attribute to normal aging or reduced fitness.</p>

<h2>Symptoms to Watch For</h2>
<p>Coughing, reduced exercise tolerance, rapid breathing at rest, and fainting or collapse episodes can all indicate heart disease.</p>

<h2>Why Early Detection Matters</h2>
<p>Earlier management can help slow progression and maintain quality of life longer compared to addressing advanced disease.</p>

<h2>Diagnosis and Monitoring</h2>
<p>A physical exam (including listening to the heart), alongside imaging and sometimes specialized cardiac testing, helps diagnose and stage heart disease.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Are certain breeds more prone to heart disease?**</strong><br>Yes, some breeds have higher genetic predisposition to specific cardiac conditions.</p>
<p><strong>**Can heart disease be managed with medication?**</strong><br>Yes, many cases are managed effectively with appropriate medication and monitoring.</p>
<p><strong>**Is coughing always heart-related?**</strong><br>No, but persistent coughing, especially in older dogs, warrants evaluation to rule out cardiac causes.</p>
