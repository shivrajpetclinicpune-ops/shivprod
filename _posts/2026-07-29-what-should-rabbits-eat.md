<p>Rabbit diet myths — mostly pellets, mostly treats, carrots as a dietary staple — are directly linked to some of the most common health problems seen in pet rabbits.</p>

<h2>The Foundation: Hay</h2>
<p>Unlimited, good-quality hay should make up the majority of a rabbit's diet — it drives dental wear and gut motility simultaneously.</p>

<h2>Vegetables and Pellets</h2>
<p>Fresh, rabbit-safe vegetables and a measured amount of quality pellets round out the diet, while sugary treats (including carrots in excess) should be limited.</p>

<h2>What to Avoid</h2>
<p>High-sugar treats, excessive pellets, and low-fiber diets are all linked to dental disease, obesity, and GI stasis.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Are carrots bad for rabbits?</strong><br>Not inherently, but they should be a small treat, not a dietary staple, due to sugar content.</p>
<p><strong>How much hay does a rabbit need daily?</strong><br>Roughly its own body size in hay volume, available at all times.</p>
<p><strong>Can diet changes cause GI stasis?</strong><br>Yes, sudden changes can disrupt gut motility — introduce changes gradually.</p>
