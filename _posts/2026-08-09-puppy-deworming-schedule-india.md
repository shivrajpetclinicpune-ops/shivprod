<p>Like kittens, puppies are commonly born with or quickly exposed to intestinal parasites, making a structured deworming schedule an early, essential part of care.</p>

<h2>Starting the Schedule</h2>
<p>Deworming typically begins around 2-3 weeks of age, repeating every 2-3 weeks through the early puppy months before transitioning to a regular adult schedule.</p>

<h2>Why Repeated Doses Matter</h2>
<p>A single dose doesn't address parasites at every life stage present in a growing puppy's system — the repeated schedule is designed around this.</p>

<h2>Signs of a Parasite Burden</h2>
<p>A pot-bellied appearance, poor growth, visible worms in stool, and diarrhea can indicate parasites needing attention.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Is deworming safe for very young puppies?</strong><br>Yes, with age-appropriate products and veterinary guidance.</p>
<p><strong>Can puppies get parasites from their mother?</strong><br>Yes, this is a common transmission route, which is why early deworming matters.</p>
<p><strong>When does deworming frequency decrease?</strong><br>Typically after the puppy series completes, transitioning to a regular adult schedule.</p>
