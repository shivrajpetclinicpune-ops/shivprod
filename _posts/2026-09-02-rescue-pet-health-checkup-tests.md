<p>Rescue pets frequently arrive with unknown, or only partially known, medical history — which shapes a somewhat more thorough initial diagnostic approach.</p>

<h2>Baseline Bloodwork</h2>
<p>Often recommended to establish a health baseline and screen for underlying conditions not evident from a physical exam alone.</p>

<h2>Infectious Disease Screening</h2>
<p>Depending on the pet's background, screening for certain infectious diseases may be recommended, particularly for pets from shelter or street environments.</p>

<h2>Parasite Testing and Treatment</h2>
<p>Fecal testing and appropriate deworming are commonly prioritized given the higher likelihood of parasite exposure in rescue situations.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Is this more extensive workup always necessary?**</strong><br>Generally recommended given unknown history, though your vet will tailor recommendations to the individual case.</p>
<p><strong>**Does this apply to both dogs and cats?**</strong><br>Yes, the same general principle applies across species with unknown history.</p>
<p><strong>**How does this affect initial cost?**</strong><br>A more thorough initial workup may cost more upfront but supports better long-term health planning.</p>
