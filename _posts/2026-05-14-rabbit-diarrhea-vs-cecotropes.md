<p>Rabbit droppings are more complex than they look — understanding the difference between normal cecotropes and true diarrhea prevents both unnecessary panic and missed emergencies.</p>

<h2>What Are Cecotropes</h2>
<p>These are soft, nutrient-rich droppings rabbits normally produce and re-ingest, usually not seen because rabbits eat them directly from the source.</p>

<h2>What True Diarrhea Looks Like</h2>
<p>Watery, loose stool that isn't the normal cecotrope pattern, especially combined with lethargy or reduced appetite, is a genuine concern.</p>

<h2>When to Worry</h2>
<p>Visible, uneaten soft droppings sticking to the rabbit, or true watery diarrhea, both warrant veterinary evaluation.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Are cecotropes a sign of illness?</strong><br>No, they're a completely normal part of rabbit digestion.</p>
<p><strong>Why would cecotropes be visible instead of eaten?</strong><br>This can indicate pain, obesity limiting reach, or dental issues preventing normal re-ingestion.</p>
<p><strong>Is true diarrhea in rabbits an emergency?</strong><br>Yes, it can indicate serious illness and dehydrates quickly.</p>
