<p>Deworming often gets framed purely as pet comfort, but it also has a genuine household health dimension worth understanding.</p>

<h2>Parasites and Human Health</h2>
<p>Certain intestinal parasites found in pets can transmit to humans, particularly children who may have closer contact with pets and outdoor areas.</p>

<h2>Why Consistency Matters</h2>
<p>A scheduled deworming routine, not just reactive treatment when symptoms appear, keeps parasite levels consistently low.</p>

<h2>Household Practices That Help</h2>
<p>Prompt waste cleanup, handwashing after pet contact, and regular veterinary deworming together form an effective combined approach.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Is this more important in households with children?**</strong><br>Yes, children often have closer, more frequent contact with pets and shared spaces, making consistent deworming particularly valuable.</p>
<p><strong>**Can I skip deworming if my pet seems healthy?**</strong><br>Not recommended — parasites aren't always visibly obvious even when present.</p>
<p><strong>**Does a clean home reduce parasite risk significantly?**</strong><br>It helps, but doesn't replace the need for scheduled veterinary deworming.</p>
