<p>"Did the X-ray find it?" is a common question after a dog swallows something — and the honest answer depends heavily on what was swallowed.</p>

<h2>What X-Rays Detect Well</h2>
<p>Dense objects like metal, bone, and stones are typically clearly visible on X-ray.</p>

<h2>What Can Be Missed</h2>
<p>Certain plastics, fabric, and some other materials may not show up clearly, even when present and causing obstruction.</p>

<h2>What Happens If X-Ray Is Inconclusive</h2>
<p>Additional diagnostics like ultrasound, or in some cases contrast studies, may be recommended if a foreign body is still suspected.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Should I still get an X-ray if I'm not sure what was swallowed?</strong><br>Yes, it's still a useful starting diagnostic step even without certainty about the object.</p>
<p><strong>Can gas patterns alone suggest an obstruction?</strong><br>Yes, abnormal gas patterns can be a helpful indirect sign even if the object itself isn't clearly visible.</p>
<p><strong>What if the X-ray is clear but symptoms continue?</strong><br>Further evaluation, including possible ultrasound, is typically recommended if symptoms persist.</p>
