<p>Kidney disease affects dogs as well as cats, though the typical age and progression pattern can differ somewhat between species.</p>

<h2>Symptoms</h2>
<p>Increased thirst and urination, appetite loss, weight loss, and lethargy are common signs, though early stages may be subtle.</p>

<h2>Diagnostic Tests</h2>
<p>Bloodwork and urine testing together are the standard approach to diagnosing and staging kidney disease.</p>

<h2>Management</h2>
<p>Treatment often involves dietary management alongside addressing any underlying or contributing causes, tailored to the individual dog's stage and situation.</p>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<p><strong>**Is kidney disease always age-related in dogs?**</strong><br>It's more common in seniors but can also result from other causes like toxin exposure or infection at any age.</p>
<p><strong>**Can kidney disease be caught before symptoms appear?**</strong><br>Yes, this is exactly why senior bloodwork screening is valuable.</p>
<p><strong>**Is diet change always part of treatment?**</strong><br>Commonly recommended, tailored to the individual dog's specific diagnosis and stage.</p>
